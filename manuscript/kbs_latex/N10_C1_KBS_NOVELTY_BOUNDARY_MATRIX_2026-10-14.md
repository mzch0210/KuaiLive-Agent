## 2. The uniquely testable knowledge-science propositions

**P1 — incumbent-relative historical complementarity.**
The target is the event-level \( \Delta_M(x; B,C)=u_{10}(M,x;C)-u_{10}(B,x;C) \), so the target changes with the fixed incumbent and candidate availability. Existing evidence: Twitch held-out 44,221 requests (Memory below Base overall yet helpful for an observable post hoc historical slice), and M3 original G5 across six fitted incumbents (Memory above Base in aggregate). **Limit**: cross-dataset contrasts have different Base configurations and ranking units, not isolated causal comparisons.

**P2 — alignment of knowledge-value supervision and deployment candidate regime.**
The relevant question is whether a gate trained on \(\Delta_F\) behaves better on actual F candidates than one trained on \(\Delta_S\), while sharing all twelve *pretarget* F descriptors and identical F roster/scoring. H1 original B4-A same-period frozen additional-target main 30,000: SASRec F−S **+0.005391** (CI [0.003646,0.007174]) and GRU4Rec **+0.003585** (CI [0.001599,0.005576]), Holm 3-hypothesis p=0.000600/0.000800. F's realized memory calls were **8,704 vs 9,105** SASRec and **8,629 vs 9,168** GRU4Rec. **Limits**: S supervises via target-retained sampled DEV candidates, so supervision labels have unequal information; natural F/S call rates differ 1.337/1.797pp, exceeding the approximate-equal-budget 1pp criterion. Do not describe a randomized equal-resource intervention.

**P3 — an authentic history matters beyond candidate competition.**
On the *same F candidate rosters*, three fixed closed-hour, author-hour marginals preserving, source-availability-valid relation-history nulls lose NDCG compared with actual Full Memory. B4-A nested 4,096 events: real **0.334942**, mean-null **0.303797**; real−mean-null **+0.031145** (CI [0.027389,0.035098]; Holm 0.000600). **Limits**: 3 seeds share a single sample; ~76.8% altered historical edges jointly change repeated and higher-order structures; predeclared same-cohort TRAIN-popularity nonfloor margin **NOT VERIFIED**; not a new future-period TEST.

**Integrated falsifiable question**: Does prediction of the *incremental value of an unchanged explicit knowledge source*, indexed by the actual ranking candidate universe, lead to improved *observable* specialist selection, and does the original source provide nontrivial value when that universe is held fixed? This is more precise than a generic claim of 'adaptive memory improves recommendation'.

## 3. Suggested revised Intro contribution paragraph (draft for C5, not yet inserted)

Our investigation treats historical viewer–creator relations as an explicitly identifiable knowledge source rather than a latent component learned jointly with the sequential recommender. We first quantify how this fixed source complements a designated incumbent, with utility measured on the actual eligible ranking alternatives. We then examine whether candidate-aligned incremental-utility supervision changes the quality and allocation of pre-outcome decisions to use that source. Finally, holding the current candidate environment fixed, we test the value of the original historical relation arrangement against time- and activity-preserving alternatives. This sequence distinguishes knowledge **valuation**, **decision**, and **source validity** from established memory-retention, feature-fusion and learning-to-defer algorithms. It characterizes the chosen knowledge source and deployment regime rather than asserting a new generic routing theorem, online economic advantage or future-time external validation.

## 4. Suggested Related Work paragraph (draft for C5, not yet inserted)

Recent memory and knowledge-aware systems make clear that conditional information use already has strong methodological precedents. Zhong et al.'s scene-aware memory discrimination method conditions decisions about **which interaction records to retain** on downstream application demands. Xie et al.'s MHGFCL integrates collaborative and structural knowledge views with adaptive feature fusion; DS-Frame allocates queries between fast and iterative slow recommenders under computation budgets. These works respectively address memory construction, knowledge representation/fusion and adaptive inference architecture. In contrast, our **fixed and explicitly auditable** relationship-history ranker permits an event-level measurement of downstream benefit over the incumbent under the current full candidate environment, making the target of knowledge-use supervision and its source-validity diagnostics directly observable. We build on established learning-to-defer principles and sampled ranking-metric analysis rather than claim to rederive them. **Cite** zhong2026samd, xie2026mhgfcl, yuan2026dsframe, krichene2020kdd plus deferral references.
