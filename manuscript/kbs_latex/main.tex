\section{Introduction}

Sequential recommenders derive user preferences from interaction sequences, with models such as SASRec and BERT4Rec learning increasingly expressive representations of recent behavior \cite{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec}. In live streaming, however, relationships between viewers and creators can persist across many ephemeral sessions. Such relationships may provide evidence beyond a trained recommender's finite input window, but they can also duplicate information already encoded in its scores. Consequently, the availability or standalone predictive strength of historical knowledge does not establish its contribution to a particular recommendation. The operative question is \emph{when a fixed relationship-memory specialist improves the ranking produced by a designated incumbent on the same eligible candidates}.

Knowledge-aware recommendation has developed mechanisms for representing, filtering and fusing historical, structural and semantic signals \cite{xie2026mhgfcl,kim2026kaper,chen2026memrec}. These advances improve how knowledge enters a recommendation model, while leaving a complementary decision problem: given a trained incumbent and an already specified auxiliary specialist, which individual requests warrant using the specialist's ranking? We operationalize its value as the specialist-minus-incumbent difference in event-level ranking utility. This definition makes knowledge value relative to the incumbent's existing capabilities and the candidates competing for the target, rather than attributing an intrinsic benefit to the relationship record alone. Established learning-to-defer methods provide the comparative allocation principle \cite{mozannar2020defer,narasimhan2022posthoc}; our focus is the ranking-specific value of a transparent historical specialist and its observable, pre-outcome prediction.

Live-streaming recommendation provides distinct conditions for examining this question. Available streamers, transient rooms and persistent creators define different prediction units and candidate sets \cite{rappaz2021liverec,qu2026kuailive}. Candidate construction can change relative ranking evaluations, as established by research on sampled recommendation metrics \cite{krichene2020sampled}. For an auxiliary source, the consequence is more specific: the same relationship evidence can yield different specialist-minus-incumbent utility when the target competes against different alternatives. Likewise, a target's historical relationship to the user may be absent from the incumbent's visible sequence without being absent from the complete prior record. These properties motivate a conditional valuation of knowledge with clearly specified history, candidate eligibility and ranking protocol.

We investigate the resulting value-to-decision chain in three complementary protocols. Twitch/LiveRec supplies a frozen streamer-level TEST of relative-utility-guided specialist choice, together with retrospective history-state diagnostics. Original KuaiLive provides a matched, previously examined room-ranking analysis in which the sampled-active and full-active candidate regimes reverse the measured Memory-minus-Base advantage. KuaiLive-M3 adds an independently frozen, globally forward-time \emph{author}-ranking TEST on 47,575 events, covering SASRec and GRU4Rec incumbents with three separately fitted training seeds each. Across this new TEST setting, five DEV-frozen selection objectives enable direct comparisons with difficulty-, quality- and uncertainty-based routing. The two held-out selection settings and the paired candidate-regime analysis answer distinct questions; their absolute NDCG values are not treated as directly comparable across ranked tasks.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incumbent-relative knowledge valuation (C1).} We measure the incremental ranking contribution of a fixed relationship-memory specialist on the incumbent's eligible candidates, characterizing heterogeneous historical complementarity and documenting opposite mean Memory-minus-Base directions across the Twitch and M3 evaluation settings.
    \item \textbf{Candidate-conditioned evaluation (C2).} We quantify a sign reversal of relative Memory utility on matched original KuaiLive room-ranking events under sampled-active and full-active candidates, with a descriptive allocation of ranked-candidate membership and score-normalization reference effects.
    \item \textbf{Frozen relative-utility-guided selection (C3).} We evaluate pre-outcome prediction of specialist-minus-Base utility as a selection signal on held-out Twitch and KuaiLive-M3 TEST events. The M3 study adds two incumbent architectures, three training seeds, four alternative DEV-frozen gate objectives, user-cluster inference and observed invocation rates, establishing decision-quality gains under specified call constraints rather than unconditional computational savings.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Historical Representation and Auxiliary Knowledge}

Sequential recommendation models learn short- and long-range behavioral dependencies from user interactions. SASRec and BERT4Rec establish self-attentive sequence representations \cite{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec}; more recent intent-aware and multi-granular systems seek to capture additional preference structure \cite{ni2025idmarec,sheng2025dsmgrec}. Improving the historical representation of an incumbent is one way to exploit persistent relationships. A complementary question concerns the incremental benefit of a separately defined history-based ranking after the incumbent has already been trained.

Knowledge-enhanced recommendation uses collaborative, structural and semantic information in a variety of forms. CKLF integrates knowledge-graph spreading activation and sequence-sensitive preference modeling \cite{chen2024cklf}. MHGFCL combines multi-head gated fusion with focused-view contrastive learning \cite{xie2026mhgfcl}, while KAPER extracts preference-relevant graph-path evidence for explainable reranking \cite{kim2026kaper}. Mutual-information-guided fusion, denoising and reliability-aware methods likewise regulate auxiliary evidence \cite{zhu2026mifusr,zhang2026mcd4sr,huo2026reliability}. MemRec constructs and distills collaborative-memory structures for LLM-based recommendation \cite{chen2026memrec}. Conditional gating has also been studied for question answering with incomplete knowledge bases \cite{yerragunta2026gating}, and research on negative transfer highlights the consequences of combining heterogeneous information \cite{peng2025negative}. These studies establish that knowledge construction and conditional integration are active research problems; our analysis fixes the knowledge specialist and trained incumbent separately, then evaluates the specialist's \emph{incremental ranking contribution} under their common candidate protocol.

This distinction also relates to the decision-theoretic value of information \cite{abbas2025voi}. The value examined here is not a representation score or a general measure of historical relevance, but the realized event-wise difference in NDCG@10 between two specified rankings. The candidate set and reference incumbent are consequently part of the operational valuation.

\subsection{Selective Prediction and Specialist Allocation}

Learning-to-Defer provides established frameworks for choosing between a predictor and an expert. Mozannar and Sontag study consistent learning to defer \cite{mozannar2020defer}, while Narasimhan and colleagues examine post-hoc expert allocation for trained predictors and its accuracy--cost tradeoff \cite{narasimhan2022posthoc}. Subsequent work considers multiple experts and expert availability \cite{mao2025mastering,montreuil2026online}; adaptive expert designs also appear in sequential recommendation \cite{sun2025cedrec}. Selecting a specialist on the basis of its expected incremental utility is therefore not, by itself, a new general decision-theoretic procedure. Our empirical target differs from the prediction-error quantities commonly examined in deferral: it is the change in top-$K$ ranking utility between two already fixed ranking outputs on identical eligible candidates. This task-specific outcome and its prospective evaluation, rather than a new optimality result for deferral, define the contribution.

The central distinction among potential selectors is their prediction target. Incumbent difficulty estimates the expected weakness of Base, specialist quality predicts the performance of the alternative in isolation, and uncertainty summarizes aspects of the incumbent's output. GUIDER, for example, adapts LLM-based recommendation using decomposed data and model uncertainty \cite{xu2026guider}. None of these signals, by definition, is identical to the expected ranking improvement from using a \emph{particular fixed} historical specialist. Our comparison measures that improvement directly and evaluates it against specified alternative selection objectives. The M3 uncertainty control is a Base top-score-margin proxy, not a reproduction of the complete GUIDER procedure.

Comparison design also matters. Twitch evaluates a DEV-frozen Utility policy against a retrospectively TEST-count-matched Difficulty selection, whereas M3 independently freezes Utility, Difficulty, Specialist Quality, Uncertainty and History pointwise thresholds on DEV. Their common \emph{DEV} invocation constraint does not force equal realized TEST call rates. The evidence accordingly concerns the quality of selecting relationship knowledge under the declared policy protocols, not a general claim of expert-routing novelty or identical deployment expense.

\subsection{Live-Streaming Recommendation and Candidate-Context Evaluation}

Live-streaming recommendation combines durable viewer preferences with time-varying opportunities. Prior models incorporate viewer--anchor interactions, room dynamics, preference histories, hypergraph structure and repeat viewing \cite{zhang2021bidirectional,gao2023driver,chen2024preference,yu2024hypergraph,zhu2025repeated}. LiveRec explicitly accounts for streamer availability and repeated consumption \cite{rappaz2021liverec}; KuaiLive distinguishes transient live rooms and persistent streamer identities \cite{qu2026kuailive}. DCGLive further models evolving collaboration among users, streamers and rooms \cite{guo2026dcglive}. These settings motivate candidate-aware evaluation of the historical knowledge carried by persistent creator identities.

Ranking metrics depend on which alternatives appear in the candidate universe. Krichene and Rendle demonstrate that metrics computed from sampled candidates can fail to preserve model comparisons under full ranking \cite{krichene2020sampled}. Our original KuaiLive analysis examines the implication for a \emph{knowledge-value contrast}: when the same target and fixed Base--Memory pair are evaluated on sampled and full-active room sets, the measured advantage of choosing Memory changes sign. Its candidate-membership/reference allocation is descriptive, since size, composition and competition vary together. The separate M3 author-ranking TEST evaluates prospective specialist choice on a full active author roster, not a second matched room-candidate intervention.

Taken together, the existing literature provides models for learning relationship representations, rules for expert allocation and warnings about candidate sampling. The present study connects these lines through a fixed-specialist, incumbent-relative ranking estimand: how much explicit relationship history adds under the actual ranking environment, and whether pre-outcome estimates of that addition improve the choice of ranking. Frozen Twitch and M3 selection studies supply decision evidence, while paired original KuaiLive results characterize candidate dependence.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

We formalize the ranking contribution of a fixed relationship specialist relative to a trained incumbent. The event-level utility difference defines a ranking-specific supervisory quantity and a decision objective under a stated invocation constraint; the constraint does not itself measure serving cost.

\subsection{Recommendation Setting and Event-Level Ranking Utility}
\label{sec:event-utility}

For user $u$ at recommendation time $t$, let the pre-outcome decision context under evaluation regime $r$ be $\xi_r=(u,t,\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t},\mathcal{H}_{u,t})$, where $\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t}$ is the candidate set under that regime and $\mathcal{H}_{u,t}$ is the interaction history observed strictly before the current decision. In the M3 forward-time protocol, previous TEST-period interactions may enter subsequent events' prequential histories after they are observed; neither current nor future event targets are available at decision time. An evaluated event is $x_r=(\xi_r,y)$, where $y\in\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t}$ is the observed relevant target. The underlying interaction instance $(u,t,y,\mathcal{H}_{u,t})$ may be held fixed while different candidate-set constructions produce distinct decision contexts and, hence, distinct full events. Ranking models receive the context but not $y$, which is used only for offline evaluation. For concision, the subscript $r$ on $\xi$ and $x$ is omitted when a regime is fixed.

Let $A$ be a ranking model and let $r_A(y\mid\xi)$ denote the 1-indexed rank assigned to $y$ among $\mathcal{C}_{u,t}$. For cutoff $K$, define the event-level ranking utility
\begin{equation}
    u_K(A,x)=
    \begin{cases}
        \displaystyle \frac{1}{\log_2\!\left(r_A(y\mid\xi)+1\right)}, & r_A(y\mid\xi)\le K,\\[6pt]
        0, & r_A(y\mid\xi)>K.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:event-ndcg}
\end{equation}
With one relevant target per event, Eq.~\eqref{eq:event-ndcg} is its NDCG@$K$ contribution; the mean across events is aggregate NDCG@$K$. Candidate construction enters the metric through the ranked alternatives, allowing the same user--target interaction to have different ranking utilities under different regimes.

\subsection{Base-Relative Specialist Utility}
\label{sec:relative-utility}

Let $B$ denote a fixed base recommender and $M$ a fixed relationship-memory specialist. For a given selective-decision experiment, both rank the same event-specific candidate set and are fixed before the invocation policy is learned. We define the specialist-minus-base utility
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_M(x)=u_K(M,x)-u_K(B,x).
    \label{eq:delta-memory}
\end{equation}
The sign of $\Delta_M(x)$ states whether the specialist improves, ties or worsens Base's ranking for the evaluated request. Thus, the measured contribution is indexed by both the Base--specialist pair and the eligible ranking candidates.

The realized $\Delta_M(x)$ is an offline supervisory quantity defined using the observed target $y$ and both candidate rankings. Decision-time selection instead relies on the expected difference given observable information. Different incumbent models, ranked units or eligible candidate regimes change the comparison itself; an aggregate sign difference between such protocols is not an isolated causal effect of any one factor.

\subsection{Conditional Relative Utility}
\label{sec:conditional-utility}

Let $R$ index the operating recommendation condition under which candidate construction, temporal availability, or related protocol elements are fixed, and let $Z$ denote the information available at invocation time. We condition on $R=r$ to describe a regime-specific event distribution; $R$ need not be an explicit feature of $Z$. The variable $Z$ contains pre-outcome history summaries and descriptors of the Base's candidate-score distribution, including score margins. These descriptors need not be calibrated uncertainty measures. Define the conditional specialist-minus-base utility as
\begin{equation}
    \eta_r(z)
    =\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_M(X)\mid Z=z,R=r\right].
    \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
Within a fixed regime we suppress $r$ when unambiguous. An estimator $\hat{\eta}_r(z)$ uses the available descriptors to predict specialist-minus-base ranking value before the event outcome.

Let $a(Z)\in\{0,1\}$ denote a pre-outcome selection policy, where $a(Z)=0$ uses the base ranking and $a(Z)=1$ uses the specialist ranking. This includes development-frozen score-threshold policies but excludes any dependence on the realized target. For an evaluated event $x$, define $u_K^{\mathrm{sel}}(x;a)=(1-a)u_K(B,x)+a\,u_K(M,x)$. The expected improvement over always using the base satisfies
\begin{align}
    &\mathbb{E}\!\left[u_K^{\mathrm{sel}}(X;a)-u_K(B,X)\mid R=r\right] \\
    &\qquad=\mathbb{E}\!\left[a(Z)\Delta_M(X)\mid R=r\right]
    =\mathbb{E}\!\left[a(Z)\eta_r(Z)\mid R=r\right].
    \label{eq:expected-selective-gain}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:expected-selective-gain} expresses the selection objective in terms of the specialist's expected incremental ranking contribution on the chosen events. The relative-expert comparison is grounded in existing learning-to-defer research \cite{mozannar2020defer,narasimhan2022posthoc}; the contribution here is its ranking- and knowledge-specific evaluation rather than a new generic decision theorem.

The policy uses $Z$ at decision time; realized relative utility, target-defined history states and target recurrence distances serve only as supervision or retrospective diagnostics. Base Difficulty characterizes the incumbent's ranking loss, whereas $\eta_r$ measures the value of choosing $M$ instead of $B$.

\subsection{Selective Invocation and Invocation-Count Constraints}
\label{sec:decision-rule}

For the Utility score \(\hat{\eta}_r(z)\), a pointwise specialist-selection policy applies a threshold \(\tau_r\) selected on DEV. Writing the protocol's frozen threshold-tie convention as \(\succeq_r\), the decision is
\begin{equation}
    a_{\tau_r}(z)=\mathbf{1}\!\left[
    \hat{\eta}_r(z)\mathrel{\succeq_r}\tau_r
    \right].
    \label{eq:utility-threshold}
\end{equation}
The M3 implementation uses an inclusive threshold and selects all events tied at the boundary. Other M3 gate objectives apply the same pointwise principle to their own frozen scores, which need not estimate \(\eta_r\).

In M3, the pointwise threshold is chosen using DEV out-of-fold predictions under \(\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{DEV}}}\sum_i a_{\tau_r}(z_i)\leq 0.30\). Once frozen, it is applied without recalibration to TEST, yielding \(\hat q_{\mathrm{TEST}}=\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{TEST}}}\sum_i a_{\tau_r}(z_i)\). The TEST rate need not remain below 30\% or match other DEV-calibrated policies. Twitch also freezes its Utility threshold, whereas its Difficulty and hindsight Oracle comparators retrospectively select the Utility policy's realized count of TEST events from the full TEST batch, rather than running their own DEV-frozen pointwise thresholds (Section~\ref{sec:exp-comparators}).

An invocation-count restriction constrains how frequently the specialist ranking is requested; it is not a direct bound on total Base scoring, history and feature computation, gate evaluation or serving latency. Accordingly, Eq.~\eqref{eq:expected-selective-gain} defines decision-quality gain under a specified call constraint, not a demonstrated reduction in deployment cost.

\subsection{Regime-Level Utility Decomposition}
\label{sec:regime-decomposition}

A change in aggregate specialist value can reflect a changed distribution of evidence states, changed state-conditional ranking value, or both. Let $S$ be an exhaustive partition of target-relative relationship states, operationalized in Section~\ref{sec:framework-states}. With $\bar{\Delta}_r=\mathbb{E}[\Delta_M(X)\mid R=r]$, total expectation gives
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\Delta}_r
    =\sum_s P(S=s\mid R=r)
    \,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_M(X)\mid S=s,R=r\right].
    \label{eq:regime-decomposition}
\end{equation}
This identity separates state prevalence from state-conditional ranking contributions. The paired candidate protocols in Section~\ref{sec:exp-regimes} then examine changes in measured relative utility while holding the evaluated target-history states fixed.

\subsection{Strong Sequential Base Recommenders}
\label{sec:framework-base}

KuaiLive uses a Dual-ID sequential Base with room-level and streamer-level SASRec branches. The room branch represents individual live sessions; the streamer branch connects sessions involving one creator. Both score the eligible \emph{rooms}, and their within-candidate standardized scores are fused by development-selected coefficients. This preserves next-room ranking while exploiting persistent streamer identity as part of the incumbent's own representation. Fusion coefficients are selected separately for the experimental candidate protocols. This room-specific within-candidate normalization is distinct from the author-level M3 scoring protocol.

For Twitch/LiveRec, Base is the availability-aware LiveRec recommender \cite{rappaz2021liverec}, including context and repeat-consumption components (input length 16). KuaiLive-M3 instead ranks active TRAIN-known authors using separately trained SASRec and GRU4Rec author encoders, each with three fitted seeds and input length 16. M3 checkpoint screening uses a sampled DEV author panel, but its final DEV and TEST utilities are measured on the full available author roster. These distinct incumbents define the respective Base-relative utility comparisons; neither the trained encoders nor the fitted selection gates are transferred across ranking tasks.

\subsection{Transparent Relationship-Memory Specialist}
\label{sec:framework-memory}

The specialist $M$ scores eligible candidates from user--creator interactions observed before the current decision. Let $i$ denote a candidate creator, let $n_{u,i}(t)$ be its count in the prior history, and let $n_{u,\max}(t)$ be the largest prior creator-specific count for user $u$. In M3, this denominator includes historical creators not present in the TRAIN-known ranking vocabulary. Define a long-term relationship strength as $\mathrm{Long}(u,i,t)=n_{u,i}(t)/\max\{1,n_{u,\max}(t)\}$. For the ordered last $K_s=10$ observed user--creator interactions, let $d$ be the reverse interaction index ($d=0$ for the most recent). In M3, past TRAIN-unseen creators also occupy recency positions despite being ineligible as ranking candidates. The short-term component is $\mathrm{Short}(u,i,t)=\max_{\{d:i_d=i\}}\exp(-d/3)$, with a maximum over an empty set defined as zero. Hence repeated appearances contribute their most recent decayed strength rather than an unbounded sum.

The popularity component $\mathrm{Pop}(i)$ is derived from eligible training-split creator counts by applying $\log(1+n_i)$ and min--max scaling over observed creators; unseen creators receive zero. The fixed specialist score is
\begin{equation}
s_M(u,i,t)=
0.45\,\mathrm{Short}(u,i,t)
+0.45\,\mathrm{Long}(u,i,t)
+0.10\,\mathrm{Pop}(i).
\label{eq:memory-scoring}
\end{equation}

The fixed scoring coefficients, short-history horizon and decay define a transparent specialist, while eligible history and ranking identity depend on the protocol. In original KuaiLive, distinct live rooms associated with one creator can share a Memory score; Twitch ranks streamers directly, and M3 ranks currently available TRAIN-known persistent authors. In M3 the last ten positions reflect \emph{all} earlier author events, and the Long normalization denominator includes the most frequently observed prior author even if unseen in TRAIN. Previously observed TEST events can update later M3 prequential histories without exposing the current or future target. A candidate lacking previous interactions may still receive a nonzero TRAIN-derived popularity score. Ranking ties follow the respective frozen deterministic conventions. Memory and the incumbent score precisely the same eligible candidates, though the incumbent's fixed input sequence need not cover the specialist's complete historical evidence (Section~\ref{sec:exp-data}).

\subsection{Base-Relative Relationship-Evidence States}
\label{sec:framework-states}

We operationalize the diagnostic variable $S$ introduced in Section~\ref{sec:regime-decomposition} using the target creator and pre-target history. Let $V_L(x)$ indicate that the target creator occurs in the sequence visible to a base with input length $L$, and let $H(x)$ indicate that the same creator occurs anywhere in the eligible pre-target relationship history. Then
\begin{equation}
S_L(x)=
\begin{cases}
\text{represented},&V_L(x)=1,\\
\text{recoverable but unrepresented},&V_L(x)=0,\ H(x)=1,\\
\text{unavailable},&V_L(x)=0,\ H(x)=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:diagnostic-states}
\end{equation}

These target-relative states describe input visibility for retrospective evaluation. In particular, \emph{represented} denotes membership in the Base input window rather than an assumption about what its trained representation has learned. The selector's observable vector $Z$ excludes $S_L$, target-specific recurrence distance and realized $\Delta_M$.

\subsection{Observable Features and Conditional Relative-Utility Estimation}
\label{sec:framework-estimation}

On Twitch, the Utility estimator $\hat{\eta}_r(z)$ is fitted on development-event $\Delta_M(x)$ labels using pre-outcome user-history features and Base candidate-score descriptors. Six history features summarize log history length, creator repeat rate, normalized preference entropy, old-versus-recent Jensen--Shannon drift, circular temporal regularity, and composite history complexity. On Twitch, regularity uses phases modulo $144$ ten-minute steps, representing a daily cycle. Eight Base-score features comprise score standard deviation and range, top-1/top-2, top-1/top-5 and top-10/top-11 margins, the top score's standardized value, normalized score entropy, and top-ten softmax mass. These quantities summarize candidate ranking patterns; entropy and softmax mass are not assumed to be calibrated uncertainty estimates.

The composite Twitch history-complexity descriptor weights percentiles of entropy, preference drift and log history length by $0.4$, $0.4$ and $0.2$.

The separate M3 estimators use 12 pre-outcome descriptors: five Base-score summaries (the top-two margin, score standard deviation, standardized top score, top score and score mean) and seven history/candidate descriptors (total earlier interaction count, earlier TRAIN-known interaction count, distinct TRAIN-known earlier authors, maximum known-author repetition, short-history distinct known authors, fraction of eligible authors with historical relationship support, and log eligible-candidate count). The support fraction is obtainable from earlier interactions and the available candidate set without evaluating the specialist's full ranking or observing the current target. This source of information is therefore permissible before the call decision. The three supervised M3 regression objectives respectively model specialist-minus-Base ranking utility, negative Base utility and standalone specialist utility; Uncertainty instead uses the negative Base score margin, while History uses total preceding interaction count. Each M3 policy is fitted and frozen on its own DEV protocol. The Twitch 14-feature and M3 12-feature vectors, as well as their trained selectors, are not asserted to be interchangeable (Section~\ref{sec:exp-freeze}).

\subsection{Specialist Selection and Comparator Policies}
\label{sec:framework-controls}

At request time, the Base supplies the default ranking and candidate-score descriptors; the selector combines them with prior-history features before applying the frozen pointwise rule in Eq.~\eqref{eq:utility-threshold}. When the policy selects Memory, its alternative ranking is computed for the same available candidates. Base and gate computations remain necessary even for requests that do not use the specialist.

Base Difficulty models incumbent ranking loss instead of the incremental benefit of using a particular specialist. The M3 comparison additionally includes Specialist Quality (standalone Memory utility), Uncertainty (negative Base margin) and History (prior interaction count), each with a separately DEV-frozen pointwise threshold. Twitch instead retains the retrospective TEST-count matching of its Difficulty and hindsight Oracle controls. Section~\ref{sec:exp-comparators} defines the different comparison designs. The reduced frequency of calling Memory is an experimentally specified decision constraint, not a measured saving in overall serving time.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

We evaluate the relative value of relationship memory through three complementary live-streaming recommendation protocols. Twitch/LiveRec provides a held-out evaluation of selective streamer ranking; the original KuaiLive task isolates candidate-regime differences on matched live-room targets; and KuaiLive-M3 provides a later-period, independently frozen evaluation of author-level specialist selection. These protocols differ in prediction unit, available candidates and evaluation design; their absolute ranking metrics are not treated as cross-task comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:exp-datasets}).

\subsection{Datasets and Prediction Tasks}
\label{sec:exp-data}

\paragraph{Twitch/LiveRec.}
The availability-aware Twitch/LiveRec protocol predicts a user's next \emph{streamer} among streamers observed as available at the prediction step \cite{rappaz2021liverec}. User--streamer activity is recorded in ten-minute crawl steps; the recorded first and last observations do not establish exact session start or end. The designated LiveRec Base uses a context length of 16. DEV and frozen TEST contain 46,878 and 44,221 one-positive ranking events. Memory uses interaction records first observed strictly before the target step and a popularity prior computed from the training period. A stricter split-end history rule is evaluated separately (Supplementary S1).

\paragraph{Original KuaiLive.}
The original KuaiLive protocol predicts a time-bounded \emph{live room} while historical relationships are represented through persistent streamer identities \cite{qu2026kuailive}. A per-user chronological leave-last-two-out split yields 10,222 eligible users with one development and one test target each. The preceding development interaction is available as history for the final test event; the popularity prior is training-only. Recorded room availability uses half-open intervals $[t_{\mathrm{start}},t_{\mathrm{end}})$. In the sampled-active condition, the target is ranked with 574 distinct contemporaneously active non-target rooms; full-active uses the contemporaneous active-room universe. The recorded target is retained if its session interval is inconsistent with its observed presence. The same 10,222 user--time--target events underpin both candidate conditions. Sampled competitors are evaluation negatives, not verified disliked rooms. This paired analysis is a retrospective candidate-regime diagnostic rather than a second prospective gate test \cite{krichene2020sampled}.

\paragraph{KuaiLive-M3.}

Eligible targets have at least two prior interactions and a TRAIN-known author; candidates are the distinct TRAIN-known authors with at least one active room at the prediction time. This forward-time author task differs from original KuaiLive room ranking; a later observation period does not establish person-disjointness across releases.

\subsection{Baselines and Compared Methods}
\label{sec:exp-comparators}

Each ranking compares a designated trained incumbent $B$ with the same fixed Short--Long--Popularity relationship-memory alternative $M$ on precisely the same event-specific eligible candidates (Section~\ref{sec:framework-memory}). The original KuaiLive Base is the fixed Dual-ID SASRec room--streamer fusion model; branch scores are standardized using each candidate universe and combined with a development-selected fusion coefficient. Its auxiliary Pop, single-branch SASRec, GRU4Rec and ContraRec--BERT4Rec checks have unequal training/tuning coverage and are not comprehensive state-of-the-art claims (Supplementary S3). Twitch uses the designated availability-aware LiveRec Base. KuaiLive-M3 uses independently trained author-level SASRec and GRU4Rec encoders, each with three prespecified training seeds (20261012, 20261013 and 20261014). In M3, Memory ranks authors from strictly earlier history using the same three conceptual components, fixed weights $0.45/0.45/0.10$, and a TRAIN-only popularity reference; unknown-to-TRAIN past authors still count in the short-history positions and long-history normalization.

The Twitch frozen selector predicts Memory-minus-Base NDCG@10 using 14 pre-outcome features. Its Base-Difficulty regression predicts $1-u_{10}(B,x)$ from the same feature family. Utility's threshold is selected on DEV and held fixed on TEST; Difficulty and the outcome-informed Oracle retrospectively choose the same number of TEST events as the frozen Utility policy, and thus are \emph{equal-count diagnostic controls}, not separately frozen pointwise TEST selectors. The old KuaiLive experiment instead compares the two fixed rankings without a second prospectively frozen routing test.

For M3, the five independently DEV-frozen pointwise selectors are \emph{Utility}, \emph{Difficulty}, \emph{Specialist Quality}, \emph{Uncertainty} and \emph{History}. Three histogram gradient-boosting regressors predict specialist-minus-Base utility, negative Base utility, and specialist utility, respectively, using the same 12 pre-outcome descriptors. These comprise five Base score summaries (margin, standard deviation, top-score standardized value, top score and score mean) and seven shared history/candidate descriptors (prior interaction count, TRAIN-known prior count, distinct prior warm authors, maximum warm-author repetition, short-history distinct count, historical support among eligible authors and log eligible-candidate count). Uncertainty ranks events by the negative Base top-score margin; History ranks by prior interaction count. The two heuristic scores do not use target-defined evidence states. These objective-specific models and thresholds are fitted separately for each frozen Base checkpoint; no trained Twitch selector is transferred to M3.

\subsection{Experimental Protocols}
\label{sec:exp-freeze}
\label{sec:exp-regimes}

\paragraph{Training, OOF estimation and policy freeze.}
All designated Base parameters are fitted without TEST outcomes, and model/checkpoint choices precede the corresponding frozen TEST evaluations. On Twitch, five-fold DEV out-of-fold (OOF) prediction provides the Utility threshold and the fitted Utility and Difficulty regressors are trained on DEV before TEST. The original room-regime analysis fixes one KuaiLive Dual-ID checkpoint pair, its sampled-development fusion coefficient and Memory score definition for both candidate universes.

For M3, 64-dimensional author-level SASRec and GRU4Rec checkpoints are trained with equal prespecified settings across the three seeds. Checkpoints are selected solely on a common 4,096-event DEV panel with 575 eligible author candidates. This screening panel is distinct from the 50,049-event \emph{full-active} DEV evaluation and the 47,575-event \emph{full-active} TEST. On the paired full-active DEV events, five user-group-disjoint OOF folds estimate each supervised gate objective, while Uncertainty and History use their specified observable scores. Each objective's pointwise score threshold is chosen to maximize DEV OOF mean selected ranking gain relative to Base, subject to at most 30\% DEV specialist invocations; all tied threshold scores are selected together. The selected thresholds and final DEV-fitted gate models are frozen before TEST. They are applied pointwise without access to TEST target outcomes, without threshold recalibration, and \emph{without imposing an additional hard cap on realized TEST invocations}. Accordingly, methods share the \emph{DEV selection constraint}, not necessarily an identical realized TEST budget. The conditional nature of DEV threshold optimization is separated from independent frozen TEST confirmation.

\paragraph{Forward-time TEST information boundary.}
In M3, author availability and Base and Memory scores are derived without the current event's target author label. The evaluation is prequential: interactions already observed before the prediction event, including earlier TEST-period interactions, may update that user's history, but the current and future targets cannot enter its feature vector, eligible ranking candidates or threshold selection. The target-blind evaluation first freezes six Base and one Memory top-10 rankings together with all five gate decisions and their checksums. The TEST target labels are then used solely to compute ranking utilities and paired inference. This distinction prevents target leakage while retaining the specified sequentially observable TEST history. The M3 cohort construction, model and threshold identities were fixed before evaluating TEST outcomes; the original prospective/retrospective scope distinctions for Twitch and old KuaiLive remain unchanged.

\paragraph{Candidate-regime comparisons.}
KuaiLive sampled-active and full-active protocols have distinct eligible candidate universes. Supplementary protocol comparisons include development-selected native configurations and a sampled-regime selector applied in the full-active setting. For the main paired ranking analysis, a single Room-SASRec/Streamer-SASRec checkpoint pair, sampled-development fusion coefficient and Memory scoring rule are held fixed. Raw scores are computed over the full-active universe and restricted to the paired sampled subset; each candidate set supplies its own branch-score standardization reference. Thus, the same user event, observed target, eligible history and fixed raw rankers are evaluated under two ranking environments. For event $i$, the paired shift is
\begin{equation}
D_i =
\big[u_{10}(M,x_i^{\mathrm{full}})-u_{10}(B,x_i^{\mathrm{full}})\big]
-
\big[u_{10}(M,x_i^{\mathrm{sampled}})-u_{10}(B,x_i^{\mathrm{sampled}})\big].
\label{eq:paired-regime-shift}
\end{equation}
The KuaiLive analysis is a paired evaluation-protocol diagnostic on the previously examined TEST cohort. Crossing ranked-candidate membership $C\in\{S,F\}$ with score-standardization reference $R\in\{S,F\}$ separates two protocol components while holding raw Base branch predictions and Memory scores fixed. With $g_i(C,R)$ the event-level Memory-minus-Base utility, the order-averaged allocations are
\begin{align}
A_{C,i}&=\tfrac12\big[g_i(F,S)-g_i(S,S)+g_i(F,F)-g_i(S,F)\big],\nonumber\\
A_{R,i}&=\tfrac12\big[g_i(S,F)-g_i(S,S)+g_i(F,F)-g_i(F,S)\big].
\label{eq:factorial-allocations}
\end{align}
By construction, $A_{C,i}+A_{R,i}=g_i(F,F)-g_i(S,S)=D_i$. This decomposition allocates the observed paired contrast within the fixed-score protocol; candidate membership combines list size, composition and competition. Three separately trained checkpoint pairs assess initialization sensitivity on the same cohort, while nine additional sampled-candidate draws are evaluated for the original fixed pair (Supplementary S5 and S8). Historical-eligibility and context-length checks examine complementary aspects of evidence availability.


\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:exp-statistics}

The primary endpoint is single-positive event NDCG@10 from Eq.~\eqref{eq:event-ndcg}; hit rate at ten (HR@10) and the realized fraction of events routed to Memory are secondary outcomes. Paired same-event contrasts evaluate Memory versus Base and Utility versus designated baselines. Target-defined relationship states (Eq.~\eqref{eq:diagnostic-states}) support retrospective conditional analyses only and never enter gate features. Twitch's post-hoc matched-call Difficulty and hindsight Oracle comparisons are distinguished from the independently frozen M3 policy outcomes; conditional Random@budget expectations and outcome-based Oracle@budget results serve solely as descriptive bounds.

Uncertainty intervals preserve pairing within users or user clusters. The primary Twitch frozen TEST comparisons use 5,000 paired bootstrap resamples; the original KuaiLive same-event candidate-regime contrasts use 3,000. For M3's 47,575 TEST events from 12,533 users, 5,000 user-cluster paired bootstrap replicates provide percentile 95\% intervals, and 5,000 user-cluster random-sign replicates provide one-sided $p$-values under an approximately sign-symmetric user-cluster null. The three predeclared comparisons are Utility versus (i) Always Base, (ii) Difficulty and (iii) Specialist Quality. Holm's sequential procedure \cite{holm1979} adjusts these \emph{three} comparisons separately within each of the six independently trained Base--seed configurations; the analysis does not assert familywise protection across all 18 correlated tests. Utility versus Uncertainty, History and unconditional Memory are additional comparisons without that preregistered primary-inference status. All six models share the same held-out TEST events, so training-seed variation is reported as fitted-model robustness rather than independent dataset replication. CIs and inference are conditional on the cohort, trained models, fixed candidate regime and frozen decision policies.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp-implementation}

The original KuaiLive Dual-ID Base uses ReChorus SASRec encoders with 64-dimensional embeddings, one attention layer, four heads and maximum history length 50. Twitch LiveRec uses context length 16 with context and repeat modules; its 14-feature histogram gradient-boosting Utility estimator uses 200 boosting iterations, learning rate $0.05$, depth $3$, minimum leaf size $50$ and $\ell_2$ penalty $1$. M3 Base encoders use 64-dimensional author embeddings and a maximum input history of 16: one causal self-attention block with four heads for SASRec, and one recurrent layer for GRU4Rec. The three supervised M3 gate objectives use the same 200-iteration, depth-3 histogram boosting configuration and group-disjoint DEV folds, separately for each trained Base. TEST rankings use contemporaneous full-active warm-author candidates and a frozen deterministic score-tie rule based on author identity. The earlier original DEV ranker and the new frozen TEST evaluator have slightly different numerical tie tolerances; neither protocol was altered after TEST outcome evaluation. The deterministic M3 cohort selection used a frozen DuckDB typed-timestamp hash implementation, whose byte identity is recorded; this differs from the original pre-execution timestamp-in-milliseconds notation, without any subsequent outcome-based cohort changes. Supplementary S1--S3 give the full split, model-selection, feature and implementation conventions, S7--S8 the decision and uncertainty details, and S9 the distinct warmed computational-cost protocols. Expert-invocation frequency is not identified with elapsed inference time or measured deployment cost.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

We first examine the incumbent-relative contribution of fixed relationship evidence in two distinct held-out ranking tasks, then evaluate whether this contribution can inform frozen specialist-selection decisions. A paired room-level candidate-regime analysis and model/feature robustness tests clarify the conditions of this decision value, followed by direct evidence on invocation constraints and scoring cost.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Utility of Relationship Memory}
\label{sec:results-heterogeneity}

The incremental value of a fixed relationship-memory specialist differs between the two held-out ranking environments. On 44,221 Twitch TEST streamer-ranking events, always using Memory yields NDCG@10 $0.53980$, compared with $0.58211$ for Base, a paired difference of $-0.04232$ (95\% CI $[-0.04521,-0.03950]$). The retrospective states defined in Section~\ref{sec:framework-states} distinguish where this specialist improves or degrades the incumbent's ranking.

Figure~\ref{fig:results-evidence-states} shows consistent state-wise directions on Twitch DEV and frozen TEST. For 5,539 \emph{recoverable-but-unrepresented} TEST targets, Memory exceeds Base by $+0.22045$ NDCG@10 on average; the represented and unavailable groups instead show differences of $-0.01935$ and $-0.18215$ (Table~\ref{tab:results-evidence-states}). The contrast identifies a substantial source of complementarity beyond the Base's input window despite the specialist's negative aggregate effect.

In the independent forward-time KuaiLive-M3 author-ranking TEST ($n=47{,}575$), the fixed Memory achieves NDCG@10 $0.34679$ against Base values from $0.25800$ to $0.26065$ across six independently fitted SASRec and GRU4Rec checkpoints. Memory's mean advantage is positive for every fit; for the primary SASRec seed it is $+0.08879$ ($0.34679-0.25800$). Thus the direction of mean Memory-minus-Base utility differs across the streamer- and author-level evaluations. The incumbent, ranked unit, candidate universe and temporal protocol also differ, so this between-task contrast does not identify a single cause of the reversal.

The Twitch evidence states are defined from the observed target and diagnose conditional utility retrospectively. The M3 mean advantage likewise establishes the value of its fixed specialist relative to the designated Base, not the advantage of a selective policy. Decision-time usefulness is assessed separately using pre-outcome features and TEST-frozen policies.

Predicting specialist-relative utility provides a decision rule for the heterogeneous value identified above. This section compares two frozen TEST selection settings, Twitch streamer ranking and KuaiLive-M3 author ranking, without pooling their absolute NDCG levels. \subsection{Candidate-Regime Dependence of Relative Utility}
\label{sec:results-regimes}

\subsection{Robustness and Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The frozen M3 TEST results are consistent across the two incumbent families and three independently trained initialization seeds within each family. Component variation offers a complementary explanation of the state contrast. Across seven locally examined Memory parameter settings on Twitch DEV, relative utility remains positive for recoverable targets and negative for represented and unavailable targets (Supplementary S4). The pattern is stable within this development sensitivity analysis.

The composition of Memory matters even within the recoverable group. Among 5,879 Twitch DEV recoverable targets, Long-only Memory attains NDCG@10 $0.63890$, versus $0.53298$ for Full Memory and $0.30201$ for Base; Long-only exceeds Full by $+0.10592$ (Supplementary Tables~S4.1--S4.2). On unavailable targets, Long-only instead trails Full by $0.03208$. These contrasting effects relate specialist advantage to the historical signal it retains; the frozen TEST specialist remains Full Memory.

The incumbent's available history also changes the comparison. Separately trained Twitch DEV Bases with input lengths $L=8,16,32$ achieve NDCG@10 $0.52619$, $0.57185$ and $0.59663$, respectively, against an unchanged Memory score of $0.52170$ (Supplementary Table~S6.1). Where targets move from recoverable to represented, Memory's relative benefit declines as the corresponding trained Base improves. Because each Base is trained separately, the contrast reflects both access to history and model adaptation.

Target--creator recurrence distance provides an additional descriptive check: the Memory-minus-Base difference varies nonmonotonically across retrospective distance bins (Supplementary Table~S6.4), consistent with the absence of a simple monotonic relationship between elapsed interaction history and ranking benefit.

These Twitch DEV diagnostics provide evidence that observable history and incumbent-score features jointly track relative Memory value, while separately trained context-length and Memory-component checks describe variation in complementarity. Additional Base competitiveness checks are reported in Supplementary S3.

Specialist invocation frequency is distinct from the cost of producing the selected ranking. The Twitch Utility policy makes 6,650 Memory calls among 44,221 frozen TEST events ($15.04\%$), whereas the six M3 DEV-frozen Utility policies invoke Memory on $27.72$--$29.21\%$ of TEST events. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Operational Value Is Base- and Context-Dependent}
\label{sec:discussion-operational-value}

The evidence distinguishes the informational content of historical relationships from their value relative to a trained recommender. In Twitch, the same transparent Memory specialist performs worse than Base on average, yet provides a large positive ranking gain for target creators present in prior interactions but absent from the incumbent's visible input. This pattern is consistent with relationship evidence complementing a truncated sequential representation on a subset of requests. On the full-active KuaiLive-M3 author task, by contrast, Memory's mean NDCG@10 exceeds each of six independently trained SASRec and GRU4Rec incumbents. The two environments thus exhibit different signs of aggregate specialist-minus-Base value while retaining an operationally meaningful event-level comparison within each task.

This contrast clarifies the unit of knowledge valuation. A specialist's standalone accuracy and its improvement over an incumbent answer different questions: the latter depends jointly on the incumbent's learned history representation, the specialist's evidence and the ranking alternatives available at the decision. Twitch and M3 change these factors together, so the difference in mean signs is not attributable to candidate count, architecture or temporal period alone. Complementarity is better interpreted at the level of a specified Base--Memory pair and ranking regime than as a universal advantage of longer relationship history. This extends the practical relevance of knowledge fusion and memory-construction research \cite{xie2026mhgfcl,chen2026memrec} by treating downstream incremental ranking value as a separately measurable outcome.

\subsection{Interpreting Conditional Evidence and Candidate Regimes}
\label{sec:discussion-candidate-regimes}

The original KuaiLive paired room-ranking analysis demonstrates why eligible candidates are part of this outcome. For unchanged user--time--target events and fixed raw Base and Memory scores, Memory-minus-Base mean utility changes from negative under 575 sampled-active rooms to positive under the corresponding full-active room universe (Section~\ref{sec:results-regimes}). The target-relative historical states remain fixed across the paired comparisons, so the observed shift cannot be explained by a changed prevalence of those states. Instead, the alternatives against which the target is ranked alter the measured contribution of the specialist.

The broader inconsistency of sampled ranking comparisons is established in prior work \cite{krichene2020sampled}. The distinct implication here concerns \emph{which knowledge source to use}: the sign of the specialist's measured incremental value, and hence the supervision available to a gate, depends on the candidate protocol. The crossed candidate-membership/score-reference analysis assigns most of the original KuaiLive shift to the membership dimension within that accounting, but membership changes list length, composition and competition together; it is not an isolated causal effect of candidate count. The distinctive implication is therefore about the candidate-conditioned label used to value a particular specialist, not a new proof of sampled-metric inconsistency. The new M3 TEST strengthens evaluation under a large full-active \emph{author} candidate universe, without constituting another paired \emph{room} candidate-size experiment.

\subsection{From Relative Utility to Selective Decisions}
\label{sec:discussion-selective-decisions}

The comparative decision principle follows established learning-to-defer theory \cite{mozannar2020defer,narasimhan2022posthoc}: choose an alternative when its expected incremental value justifies doing so under the specified decision rule. The relevant prediction target for relationship knowledge is the NDCG@10 gain of \emph{this} Memory specialist over \emph{this} incumbent, not merely whether the incumbent is likely to fail. An event can be difficult for Base without becoming better ranked by Memory, and a strong Memory ranking need not represent a gain over an equally strong Base. Historical context and Base score descriptors together provide pre-outcome information with which to estimate that distinction.

The frozen Twitch TEST demonstrates improved selection relative to Base and to a retrospectively equal-count Difficulty control. KuaiLive-M3 adds a separately frozen author-ranking evaluation: across two Base families and three fitted seeds each, Utility surpasses Base and four DEV-frozen alternative gate objectives. In the M3 primary comparisons, Utility's gains over Base, Difficulty and Specialist Quality have positive user-cluster intervals and survive the three-comparison Holm procedure within every fitted Base--seed condition (Section~\ref{sec:results-decision}). The strongest M3 alternative, a simple Base-margin Uncertainty score, remains below Utility, although it is a secondary comparison and does not reproduce GUIDER's uncertainty model \cite{xu2026guider}. The six fitted models share the same TEST cohort and therefore provide model-initialization and architecture sensitivity evidence rather than six independent population replications.

The results support the discriminative value of predicting relative ranking improvement without requiring perfect event-wise utility prediction. On Twitch, the selected-event gain is positive despite a modest realized-utility rank correlation and a substantial gap to the outcome-informed Oracle. On M3, the corresponding frozen selection gains persist across the fitted incumbents, but the Oracle@budget remains a retrospective upper bound. This evidence identifies an actionable \emph{decision signal}, not an optimal selector for every feasible information or resource condition.

\subsection{Invocation Constraints, Computational Cost and Validity}
\label{sec:discussion-validity}

The interpretation of selective decisions depends on the resource being constrained. The M3 policies have independently selected thresholds under a common maximum 30\% \emph{DEV} Memory-call criterion; their TEST invocation rates need not be equal or bounded by 30\%. The original Twitch Difficulty and Oracle controls instead match the realized frozen Utility count retrospectively. Both designs permit conclusions about the stated comparison, but neither establishes a universally enforced online quota or equal measured compute cost. The distinction is material because Base scoring, history-feature construction and gate inference contribute computation even when Memory is not invoked.

The M3 results also identify a concrete boundary for deployment. Fixed Always Memory reaches NDCG@10 $0.34679$, above the six Utility policies ($0.30177$--$0.30400$). In the separate 256-event warmed M3 DEV replay, Always Memory averages $0.803$\,ms per event versus $1.802$\,ms for Utility routing. Hence, under the measured precision and warmed scoring conditions, choosing the selective path is not justified as either more accurate or faster than simply using Memory. A specialist-call restriction could matter when knowledge access incurs an independently established external cost, permission requirement or resource limit; no such operational scarcity is measured here. The study therefore establishes \emph{selection quality under a stipulated call constraint}, not a general claim that a gate is economically preferable or that fewer specialist calls reduce serving latency.

Evaluation scope further conditions generalization. Twitch and M3 rank streamers and authors, whereas original KuaiLive ranks live rooms; their candidates, incumbent architectures and history construction differ. M3 uses a global forward-time TEST with prequentially observed earlier TEST interactions available to subsequent requests, not a person-disjoint cross-platform holdout. Models and pointwise policies are frozen before current TEST outcomes, while original KuaiLive candidate comparisons and target-defined relationship states are retrospective diagnostics. The M3 user-cluster intervals and within-fit Holm adjustments support the prespecified comparisons on this shared cohort, not a single globally adjusted family of 18 independent tests. Finally, warmed offline timing omits history maintenance, candidate-availability construction and production scheduling. These scope distinctions determine which claims the three protocols establish, rather than altering the observed improvements.

\subsection{Implications for Knowledge-Based Recommendation}
\label{sec:discussion-implications}

The combined findings suggest a decision-oriented way to evaluate additional relational evidence. A knowledge specialist should be assessed against a designated trained incumbent on the same time-eligible candidates, with standalone specialist quality and incumbent-relative improvement reported separately. Conditional analysis can then identify when earlier interactions provide complementary ranking information, while comparisons across candidate protocols test whether that evaluation remains appropriate for the intended ranking task. These steps make the contribution of historical knowledge empirically interpretable beyond its representation in a neural encoder.

Where an expert-invocation restriction is substantively justified, a selector should be trained for the specialist's predicted incremental utility rather than assuming that Base difficulty, standalone expert quality or an uncertainty proxy answers the same allocation question. Reporting frozen TEST call rates alongside ranking metrics and the complete observed computation path is necessary to assess whether such routing improves a real system. The present evidence supports relative-utility-aware decision evaluation for fixed relationship specialists, while indicating that unconditional Memory may be the better choice when no genuine invocation constraint applies.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluates historical relationship knowledge by its incremental ranking contribution relative to a designated trained recommender and the eligible candidate set. In Twitch, fixed relationship Memory underperforms the incumbent on average but produces substantial gains for retrospectively identifiable recoverable relationships. In the distinct KuaiLive-M3 author-ranking evaluation, Memory instead exceeds six fitted incumbent models in aggregate. These findings locate the value of relationship evidence in a particular Base--specialist comparison, rather than in historical information alone.

Pre-outcome prediction of this relative value supports selective ranking decisions. A DEV-frozen Utility policy improves held-out Twitch ranking over Base and a retrospectively matched Difficulty control; in the independent forward-time M3 TEST, Utility outperforms four other DEV-frozen selection objectives across SASRec and GRU4Rec fits. The original paired KuaiLive room experiment shows why this value must be evaluated under the relevant candidate environment: sampled-active and full-active candidate protocols reverse the measured Memory advantage for unchanged target events. Together, these results connect the conditional value of relationship knowledge with the decision to use it.

The resulting contribution is a ranking- and candidate-aware evaluation of fixed relational evidence and invocation-constrained decision quality. It does not imply that selective routing is universally preferable: on M3, unconditional Memory achieves higher absolute ranking quality and lower measured warmed scoring time than the selective path. Distinguishing expert-call constraints from actual system costs is therefore essential when translating relative-utility predictions into deployment decisions.
