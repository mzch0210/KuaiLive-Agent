\title{When Does Relationship Memory Help? Base-Relative Evidence Valuation for Live-Streaming Recommendation}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Persistent viewer--creator relationships may complement sequential recommenders, but their contribution depends on the trained incumbent and the candidates available for ranking. We quantify the event-level NDCG@10 gain of a fixed Short--Long--Popularity relationship-memory specialist over a trained Base on identical eligible candidates, and estimate that incremental value from decision-time history and Base-score descriptors. On 44,221 held-out Twitch streamer-ranking events, a development-frozen utility selector increases NDCG@10 from 0.58211 to 0.58983 while invoking Memory on 15.04\% of requests. For 10,222 paired KuaiLive room-ranking events, Memory-minus-Base utility reverses from $-0.04120$ under sampled-active candidates to $+0.04631$ under full-active candidates, revealing the ranking-context dependence of measured knowledge value. In a subsequent exploratory analysis of the same 50,049 M3 development events, full-candidate rather than sampled-candidate utility supervision improves held-out full-ranking decision NDCG@10 by 0.00616 for SASRec and 0.00497 for GRU4Rec, changing the particular requests selected for Memory on 21.3\% and 23.0\% of events, respectively. Source value also depends on candidate context; in 1,024 exploratory M3 DEV requests, time- and activity-preserving history randomization lowers full-candidate Memory NDCG@10 by 0.0329--0.0355 across three fixed nulls. In a separately frozen KuaiLive-M3 author-ranking TEST, relative-utility selection achieves higher NDCG@10 with fewer observed Memory calls than four specified alternative selection objectives across six fitted incumbents. Nevertheless, unconditional Memory achieves higher absolute M3 NDCG@10 (0.34679) and lower warmed development-replay scoring time than selective routing. The findings establish incumbent- and candidate-relative relationship value and demonstrate its decision relevance under stated invocation constraints, while separating specialist-call allocation from operational efficiency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sequential recommenders infer preferences from behavioral sequences, improving the modeling of recent and recurrent interests through architectures such as SASRec and BERT4Rec \cite{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec}. In live-streaming services, however, the objects available for recommendation change with ongoing broadcasts, while viewer--creator relationships may persist across many sessions. A separately maintained relationship history can recover evidence outside a model's input sequence, but it may also repeat information already represented by the trained recommender. This creates a knowledge-use question distinct from learning a richer representation: \emph{what additional ranking value does a specified relationship-memory specialist provide beyond the incumbent for the candidates available at the decision?}

The answer must be comparative. A high-scoring memory-based recommender need not improve every request over a strong incumbent, and a difficult request need not be one on which that particular specialist helps. We therefore study \emph{incumbent-relative relationship value}, measured as the event-level difference in top-$K$ ranking utility between a fixed Memory specialist and a trained Base on the same eligible candidates. The sign and magnitude of this value can vary with the user's historical relationships, the incumbent's ranking context, and the eligible alternatives. Predicting its conditional expectation using information available before the target outcome provides a principled input to the specialist-selection decision. Expert allocation itself has a mature learning-to-defer foundation \cite{mozannar2020defer,narasimhan2022posthoc,mao2024regression}; our focus is the ranking-specific valuation and empirical decision relevance of an explicitly defined historical-knowledge specialist.

Two features of live recommendation make this valuation especially informative. First, a relationship that the incumbent does not encounter in its recent input may remain available in the user's older observations; its eventual relevance can be identified in retrospective outcome analysis without presuming that target identity is visible at decision time. Second, candidate sets change with live availability. Because ranking utility depends on the competitors, the same fixed Base--Memory pair may have a different measured advantage when eligible rooms or creators change. This is related to the established sensitivity of sampled recommendation metrics to the evaluated candidate universe \cite{krichene2020kdd}; here it raises a further question about the meaning of the \emph{knowledge-value supervision target} used by a selector.

These considerations define one connected research problem: characterize when a fixed relationship specialist adds ranking utility to its incumbent, determine whether a pre-outcome estimate of that increment can guide specialist use under an explicitly specified invocation-count constraint, and examine how the candidate regime shapes the value being estimated. We investigate these aspects in three complementary live-streaming protocols rather than treating them as interchangeable replications. Twitch/LiveRec tests streamer-level selection and retrospectively characterizes historical complementarity; original KuaiLive provides a paired, previously examined live-\emph{room} candidate-regime comparison; and KuaiLive-M3 evaluates author-level decisions on a separately frozen forward-time TEST cohort across independently trained SASRec and GRU4Rec incumbents \cite{rappaz2021liverec,qu2026kuailive}.

Across two frozen selection tests, relative-utility guidance improves ranking compared with the specified alternative decision rules under their respective invocation designs. The paired original KuaiLive room evaluation demonstrates a sign reversal of measured Memory--Base value across candidate regimes. A subsequent matched development-set study links this candidate-dependent supervision directly to downstream knowledge-use decisions and examines the information sources responsible for the specialist's utility. Unconditional Memory remains a strong M3 reference when no specialist-call constraint is imposed.

Our contributions form a cumulative account of relationship knowledge as a \emph{conditional ranking resource}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incumbent-relative relationship valuation.} We operationalize the marginal event-level ranking value of a fixed, transparent relationship-memory specialist relative to trained sequential recommenders, and characterize heterogeneity in its historical complementarity across two held-out live-ranking tasks.
    \item \textbf{Decision relevance of predicted marginal value.} We test specialist selection informed by pre-outcome relative-utility estimates in frozen Twitch and KuaiLive-M3 evaluations. Across the six M3 incumbent fits, Utility improves ranking quality while making fewer observed Memory calls than each of four specified alternative selection objectives, under a common DEV calibration constraint.
    \item \textbf{Candidate-conditioned interpretation of knowledge value.} A paired original KuaiLive analysis shows that the measured gain of the fixed specialist can reverse between sampled-active and full-active room candidates. Matched exploratory M3 development experiments show that supervision alignment redistributes specialist calls across 21--23\% of requests and changes their ranking value. Paired source comparisons reveal candidate-dependent evaluation, while fixed-candidate, time- and activity-preserving relation-identity controls show that the historical arrangement itself carries ranking information under the tested perturbation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Sequential Recommendation and Relationship Knowledge}

Sequential models learn representations of evolving behavioral preferences through attention, recurrence and multi-interest structure \cite{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec,ni2025idmarec,sheng2025dsmgrec}. Knowledge-aware approaches extend these representations through additional relational or multimodal evidence. CKLF combines knowledge-graph propagation with sequence-sensitive preference modeling \cite{chen2024cklf}; MHGFCL adaptively fuses collaborative, semantic and structural views using multi-head gating \cite{xie2026mhgfcl}; KAPER selects graph-path evidence for preference-sensitive reranking \cite{kim2026kaper}. Such work demonstrates the value of learning how auxiliary information should enter the recommender. The question addressed here is complementary: given a trained incumbent and a separately specified relationship ranker, how should the \emph{marginal benefit} of that available knowledge be measured and predicted on an individual ranking event?

The distinction is also relevant to memory-augmented recommendation. MemRec builds collaborative memory and distills information for an LLM-based recommender \cite{chen2026memrec}, while reliability- and denoising-oriented fusion models seek to control noisy or redundant auxiliary signals \cite{peng2025negative,zhu2026mifusr,zhang2026mcd4sr}. Our specialist instead remains fixed and transparent, allowing each observed gain to be defined relative to the incumbent's unchanged scores and the event's eligible alternatives. This operational notion is related to decision-theoretic information value \cite{abbas2025voi}: information matters through its consequence for a particular decision, not simply through its availability or richness. Here that consequence is a change in top-ten ranking utility for two fixed outputs.

\subsection{Learning to Defer and Adaptive Specialist Selection}

Learning-to-Defer studies when a prediction should be delegated to a complementary expert. Mozannar and Sontag develop consistent deferral estimators \cite{mozannar2020defer}; Narasimhan et al.\ train post-hoc decision rules over an existing predictor to address quality--cost tradeoffs \cite{narasimhan2022posthoc}. Mao et al.\ formulate single- and two-stage regression deferral for multiple experts, including bounded losses and instance-dependent costs \cite{mao2024regression}, and later study stronger guarantees for multiple-expert routing \cite{mao2025mastering}. These results supply the established foundations for a selector based on conditional relative performance. Our study does not require a new generic deferral theorem: it evaluates a fixed historical specialist using \emph{candidate-aligned event-level NDCG differences} and investigates the resulting supervised value signal in sequential recommendation.

Adaptive routing has also entered recommendation models directly. DS-Frame combines a fast sequential recommender, an iterative slow latent-refinement system, and a learned selector under a controllable computation budget \cite{yuan2026dsframe}. Its adaptive inference architecture and computational objectives provide a close precedent for budgeted recommendation routing. We instead hold a transparent relationship-history ranker separate from the incumbent, measure the gain attributable to choosing its ranking, and analyze how historical eligibility and candidate composition shape that gain. Thus the central contribution is the empirical \emph{valuation-to-decision relationship} for fixed knowledge, rather than the general ability to route to an expensive recommendation model.

Different selection scores test different information about the same decision. Predicting Base difficulty measures the incumbent's likely loss; predicting standalone Memory quality measures the specialist's likely success; and score-margin heuristics describe the incumbent's output structure. More elaborate uncertainty-guided recommender methods such as GUIDER use a different LLM-based uncertainty formulation \cite{xu2026guider}. Our frozen policy comparisons operationalize these distinct targets within the same Base--Memory ranking experiment, with the M3 uncertainty baseline explicitly defined as a Base score-margin heuristic. The comparison of these decision targets is therefore conducted using the same designated Base--Memory pair and a clearly specified ranking-event outcome, rather than treating heuristic margins or standalone accuracy as interchangeable notions of specialist value.

\subsection{Live-Streaming Ranking and Candidate-Dependent Evaluation}

Live recommendation couples persistent viewer interests with time-varying availability, room identity and repeat engagement. LiveRec accounts for live streamer availability and recurrent consumption \cite{rappaz2021liverec}; KuaiLive provides room sessions linked to persistent streamer identities \cite{qu2026kuailive}; and DCGLive studies collaboration involving users, rooms and creators \cite{guo2026dcglive}. Work on repeated viewing and time-dependent live behavior further illustrates why historical preferences and available alternatives cannot be assumed to be static \cite{gao2023driver,zhu2025repeated}. These settings supply distinct ranked units: streamers on Twitch, rooms in the original KuaiLive protocol, and persistent authors in our M3 author-ranking evaluation.

Candidate composition also changes the meaning of ranking metrics. Krichene and Rendle show that sampled ranking evaluations can fail to preserve full-ranking model comparisons \cite{krichene2020kdd}. Building on this established evaluation issue, we examine a specific fixed-expert consequence: the \emph{specialist-minus-incumbent} utility label itself changes across paired live-room candidate regimes even when the evaluated interactions and raw scoring functions are held fixed. The paired accounting distinguishes candidate membership from the Base score-normalization reference, while leaving list size, composition and rank competition jointly embedded in the membership effect. The full-active M3 selection TEST and the original room-regime diagnosis accordingly provide different, complementary forms of evidence.

Together, these literatures establish the distinct precedents for learning representations of auxiliary knowledge, choosing among fixed experts, and candidate-sensitive ranking evaluation. Their intersection motivates a further empirical question: when a relative-utility target for explicit historical knowledge is estimated under one candidate regime, does that supervision remain decision-useful under the candidate regime in which the specialist will actually be used? We address this question on matched full-active M3 author-ranking development events, and examine whether the historical source and observable feature set change that supervision-to-decision relationship. The contribution is evidence about a candidate-indexed knowledge valuation target and its consequences for decisions, not the invention of expert deferral or sampled-metric inconsistency.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let the value of a relationship-memory specialist be the change in event-level ranking utility obtained by replacing the designated trained incumbent on the same eligible candidates. This definition supplies an observable supervision target and a candidate-regime-specific basis for interpretation.

\subsection{Recommendation Setting and Event-Level Ranking Utility}
\label{sec:event-utility}

For user $u$ at decision time $t$ and evaluation regime $r$, let $\xi_r=(u,t,\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t},\mathcal{H}_{u,t})$ denote the pre-outcome context. Here $\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t}$ is the eligible candidate set and $\mathcal{H}_{u,t}$ contains interactions already observed before the decision. An evaluated event is $x_r=(\xi_r,y)$, where $y\in\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{u,t}$ is the subsequently observed relevant item. Both rankers operate on $\xi_r$ without access to $y$. The regime indexes the construction of candidates and ranking scores; the same user--time--target interaction may therefore be evaluated under distinct candidate regimes. We omit $r$ when the regime is fixed.

Let $r_A(y\mid\xi)$ be the one-indexed position of $y$ in the ranking produced by model $A$. For a single observed relevant target, event-level NDCG@$K$ is
\begin{equation}
    u_K(A,x)=
    \begin{cases}
        \displaystyle \frac{1}{\log_2\!\left(r_A(y\mid\xi)+1\right)}, & r_A(y\mid\xi)\le K,\\[6pt]
        0, & r_A(y\mid\xi)>K.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:event-ndcg}
\end{equation}
Its mean across a specified set of events is the corresponding aggregate NDCG@$K$. The realized target determines the offline utility, whereas the eligible candidates determine the competition in which that target is ranked. We use $K=10$ in the experiments.

\subsection{Base-Relative Specialist Utility}
\label{sec:relative-utility}

Let $B$ be the designated trained incumbent and $M$ a fixed relationship-memory specialist within an evaluation protocol. Each assigns a ranking to the \emph{same} candidate set for the same event. The event-level incremental utility of invoking the specialist is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_M(x)=u_K(M,x)-u_K(B,x).
    \label{eq:delta-memory}
\end{equation}
Positive, zero and negative values indicate that Memory improves, ties or worsens the incumbent's rank utility, respectively. Although $M$ may use relationship history beyond the incumbent's visible input, its usefulness is defined by the particular $B$, target-ranking unit and candidates. Since $\Delta_M$ depends on the observed target, it is available for supervised development and retrospective analysis, but not at the moment of selection.

\subsection{Conditional Relative Utility}
\label{sec:conditional-utility}

Let $Z$ comprise decision-time observable summaries of user history and the Base's candidate scores. For the event distribution associated with regime $R=r$, define
\begin{equation}
    \eta_r(z)
    =\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_M(X)\mid Z=z,R=r\right].
    \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
The estimator $\hat{\eta}_r(z)$ predicts the ranking gain of using the fixed specialist in place of its incumbent. Conditioning on the regime makes the candidate and ranking protocol part of the estimation target; it does not require $R$ to be a separately provided input feature.

For the paired development evaluations considered below, $\Delta_S(x)$ and $\Delta_F(x)$ denote the specialist-minus-incumbent utility obtained on the sampled and full-active candidate sets, respectively. For a pre-outcome selection policy $\pi$, let $V_F(\pi)=\mathbb{E}[u_{10}(B,X_F)+\pi(Z)\Delta_F(X)]$ be its expected utility when the actual evaluation regime is full-active. The candidate-supervision transfer contrast is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{S\rightarrow F}
    =V_F(\pi_F)-V_F(\pi_S),
    \label{eq:candidate-supervision-transfer}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_F$ and $\pi_S$ are fitted and calibrated with full-active and sampled-candidate utility labels, respectively. This is an empirical difference between fitted policies evaluated on the same full-active events; its sign is not asserted to be universally positive, and unequal realized specialist calls must be reported.

A pre-outcome decision $a(Z)\in\{0,1\}$ returns the Base ranking when $a=0$ and invokes the Memory ranking when $a=1$. Its realized event utility is $u_K^{\mathrm{sel}}(x;a)=(1-a)u_K(B,x)+a\,u_K(M,x)$. By iterated expectation,
\begin{align}
    &\mathbb{E}\!\left[u_K^{\mathrm{sel}}(X;a)-u_K(B,X)\mid R=r\right] \\
    &\qquad=\mathbb{E}\!\left[a(Z)\Delta_M(X)\mid R=r\right]
    =\mathbb{E}\!\left[a(Z)\eta_r(Z)\mid R=r\right].
    \label{eq:expected-selective-gain}
\end{align}
This identity connects the relative-value prediction target to selective ranking quality. The general idea of allocating predictions to an expert is established in learning-to-defer research \cite{mozannar2020defer,narasimhan2022posthoc}; our analysis operationalizes that comparison for a fixed relationship-memory ranker and event-level candidate-dependent ranking utility.

\subsection{Selective Invocation and Invocation-Count Constraints}
\label{sec:decision-rule}

A score-based policy selects Memory when its estimated relative utility exceeds a development-calibrated threshold. Denoting the protocol's deterministic boundary comparison by $\succeq_r$, the Utility policy is
\begin{equation}
    a_{\tau_r}(z)=\mathbf{1}\!\left[
    \hat{\eta}_r(z)\mathrel{\succeq_r}\tau_r
    \right].
    \label{eq:utility-threshold}
\end{equation}
Among candidate thresholds, development data determine a selection rule with a specified maximum fraction of Memory invocations. The finite-sample constraint is $\hat{q}_{\mathrm{DEV}}=n_{\mathrm{DEV}}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{DEV}}}a_{\tau_r}(z_i)\le q_r$, where $q_r$ is the design limit; it is not a guaranteed upper bound on the realized future invocation rate. The threshold-selection and comparator-specific procedures are given in Section~\ref{sec:exp-freeze}. This is an \emph{invocation-count} constraint rather than a measured budget on end-to-end computation.

\subsection{Regime-Level Utility Decomposition}
\label{sec:regime-decomposition}

To interpret aggregate differences, let $S$ index mutually exclusive target-relative relationship-evidence states (Section~\ref{sec:framework-states}). Their contributions to mean specialist value satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\Delta}_r
    =\sum_s P(S=s\mid R=r)
    \,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta_M(X)\mid S=s,R=r\right],
    \label{eq:regime-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\Delta}_r=\mathbb{E}[\Delta_M(X)\mid R=r]$. This separates the prevalence of retrospectively defined states from their state-conditional utility; the paired candidate-regime contrast is defined in Section~\ref{sec:exp-regimes}.

\label{sec:framework}

The framework connects a trained sequential incumbent, an explicit fixed relationship-memory specialist and a selector that estimates their relative ranking value (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}). Base scores and historical descriptors are available before the choice; the Memory ranking is computed on selected requests. Observed outcomes subsequently provide development supervision and retrospective explanations of relative value.

\subsection{Strong Sequential Base Recommenders}
\label{sec:framework-base}

The Base represents the recommendation capability to which relationship knowledge is added. The original KuaiLive Base combines room- and creator-identity SASRec branches to rank eligible \emph{rooms}; within-candidate score standardization precedes their development-selected fusion. Twitch uses the availability-aware LiveRec model with context and repeated-consumption components \cite{rappaz2021liverec}. KuaiLive-M3 employs author-level SASRec and GRU4Rec encoders that rank eligible persistent authors. These are designated trained incumbents for their respective tasks rather than a single model transferred across datasets. Their training, candidate construction and checkpoint selection are specified in Section~\ref{sec:experimental-setup} and Supplementary S2--S3.

\subsection{Transparent Relationship-Memory Specialist}
\label{sec:framework-memory}

The specialist scores each eligible candidate using user--creator relationships observed strictly before the current event. Let $n_{u,i}(t)$ be the number of prior user interactions with creator $i$, and let $n_{u,\max}(t)=\max_j n_{u,j}(t)$ over the eligible recorded history. The long-term relationship score is $\mathrm{Long}(u,i,t)=n_{u,i}(t)/\max\{1,n_{u,\max}(t)\}$. For the last $K_s=10$ user--creator interactions, indexed backward from the most recent by $d=0$, the short-term score is $\mathrm{Short}(u,i,t)=\max_{\{d:i_d=i\}}\exp(-d/3)$, with an empty-set maximum of zero. Repeated recent interactions thus contribute their most recent decayed strength. The popularity score $\mathrm{Pop}(i)$ applies log-count transformation and min--max scaling to the training-split creator frequencies, assigning zero to a creator absent from that reference. The fixed relationship ranking score is
\begin{equation}
s_M(u,i,t)=
0.45\,\mathrm{Short}(u,i,t)
+0.45\,\mathrm{Long}(u,i,t)
+0.10\,\mathrm{Pop}(i).
\label{eq:memory-scoring}
\end{equation}

These weights define the fixed specialist rather than a per-event trained fusion. In the original room-ranking task, multiple eligible rooms can inherit a common creator relationship score. Twitch and M3 directly rank persistent streamers or authors. In M3, the last ten \emph{all-author} prior interactions determine Short, while Long's normalization counts all past authors, including authors absent from the TRAIN-known candidate vocabulary. Such earlier authors can affect historical recency and normalization without becoming eligible current candidates. The frozen incumbent and Memory always rank the same decision-time candidate set; deterministic ranking-tie conventions and dataset-specific history eligibility are documented in Section~\ref{sec:exp-data} and Supplementary S1--S2.

\subsection{Base-Relative Relationship-Evidence States}
\label{sec:framework-states}

The visibility of the subsequently observed target in prior history provides an interpretable partition of realized specialist value. Let $V_L(x)$ indicate whether the target creator occurs within the Base's most recent $L$ input interactions and $H(x)$ whether it occurs anywhere in the eligible pre-target history. Define
\begin{equation}
S_L(x)=
\begin{cases}
\text{represented},&V_L(x)=1,\\
\text{recoverable but unrepresented},&V_L(x)=0,\ H(x)=1,\\
\text{unavailable},&V_L(x)=0,\ H(x)=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:diagnostic-states}
\end{equation}
Here \emph{represented} describes input-sequence membership, not a claim about what the trained Base representation retains. Since this partition refers to the observed target, it is used to explain results after evaluation; neither $S_L$ nor target-specific recurrence distance is a selector input.

\subsection{Observable Features and Conditional Relative-Utility Estimation}
\label{sec:framework-estimation}

The decision vector $Z$ combines historical relationship descriptors with statistics computed from the Base's candidate scores. Its history component characterizes how much and what kind of repeated creator evidence is available, while score-distribution summaries characterize the incumbent's current ranking context. The relative-utility regressor is trained on DEV event labels $\Delta_M(x)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-memory}); the current target, its diagnostic state and realized rank utility are excluded from the predictor features.

The Twitch implementation uses six history and eight Base-score descriptors; M3 uses seven history/candidate and five Base-score descriptors. The platforms use different history construction and ranking units, so their fitted models and feature vectors are protocol-specific. In M3, the history-support fraction can be obtained from previously observed creator identities and the available candidate roster \emph{before} evaluating the full Memory ranking; it does not expose the current target. Section~\ref{sec:exp-comparators} defines the comparator prediction targets and exact feature groups, with full transformations in Supplementary S2 and S7.

\subsection{Specialist Selection and Comparator Policies}
\label{sec:framework-controls}

At inference, the Base produces the default ranking and its observable score descriptors. The selector applies the DEV-calibrated pointwise rule from Eq.~\eqref{eq:utility-threshold}, returning the Base ranking or computing Memory on the identical available candidates. To test whether specialist-specific value offers more decision information than a broader request-difficulty signal, we compare Utility against predictors of incumbent difficulty and standalone specialist performance, as well as pre-outcome heuristic policies where the experimental protocol permits. Their complete definitions and inferential roles belong to Section~\ref{sec:exp-comparators}. The selection mechanism changes which ranking is returned, not the two underlying fixed rankings.


\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

Table~\ref{tab:exp-datasets} defines the distinct ranked units, candidate sets and evaluation roles of the three protocols. All comparisons are evaluated within their designated task and candidate environment.

\subsection{Datasets and Prediction Tasks}
\label{sec:exp-data}

\paragraph{Twitch/LiveRec.}
We use the availability-aware LiveRec protocol to rank streamers at ten-minute observed activity steps \cite{rappaz2021liverec}. The filtered study has 46,878 DEV and 44,221 held-out TEST requests, each with one recorded relevant streamer. The Base has an input length of 16; eligible relationships precede the target step, and the Memory popularity reference uses training records. Recorded observation boundaries are not equated with physical session entry or exit times (Supplementary S1).

\paragraph{Original KuaiLive.}
We rank a time-bounded live \emph{room} using a Dual-ID Base and a specialist that aggregates histories through persistent creators \cite{qu2026kuailive}. A per-user chronological leave-last-two-out split yields 10,222 eligible users with paired development and previously examined test events. In the sampled-active regime, each target is ranked alongside 574 distinct contemporaneously active non-target rooms; the full-active regime includes the contemporaneous room roster. Session eligibility uses recorded half-open intervals; the observed positive is retained if its recorded interval conflicts with the event. The 575 candidates include the target, and sampled non-targets are ranking alternatives rather than verified negative preferences. The paired candidate analysis reuses the same 10,222 previously examined TEST targets.

\paragraph{KuaiLive-M3.}
The M3 evaluation ranks persistent \texttt{author\_id} identities; contemporaneously active room sessions define author availability. Its global recorded-time TRAIN boundary is 23 March 2026 12:00, followed by DEV until 27 March 2026 14:00 and TEST thereafter, using source ordering without assigning an unverified time zone. From 24,995,148 eligible training interactions, the protocol builds a deterministic, outcome-independent evaluation cohort of 50,049 DEV and 47,575 TEST requests, with 12,533 users in TEST. Each eligible target is TRAIN-known and has at least two prior valid interactions; candidates are distinct TRAIN-known authors with at least one active room at decision time. TEST uses the complete eligible author roster (median 31,065 candidates). The 4,096-request, 575-author DEV panel is used only for neural checkpoint screening, not final full-active evaluation. The author task and global forward-time split are distinct from original KuaiLive room prediction; the protocol does not assert user-disjointness between temporal segments.

\subsection{Baselines and Compared Methods}
\label{sec:exp-comparators}

Within every task, the two fixed scorers rank the same available candidates, and Utility predicts their single-event difference (Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-memory}). Twitch uses the trained LiveRec Base and the fixed 14-feature Utility regressor. Its \emph{Difficulty} comparator predicts $1-u_{10}(B,x)$ from the same decision-time feature family. The Utility threshold is DEV-frozen; for evaluation, Difficulty and the outcome-based Oracle choose their top-scored or highest-realized-gain requests at the \emph{retrospectively matched TEST call count}. These equal-count comparisons measure selection quality but are not separately frozen pointwise deployment policies.

The original KuaiLive candidate analysis uses the same fixed Dual-ID Base checkpoint pair, sampled-DEV-selected room fusion coefficient and fixed Memory scoring across the paired sampled/full candidate regimes. Its separate baseline-competitiveness tests use additional model configurations with different tuning budgets (Supplementary S3), rather than a claim of equivalently optimized state-of-the-art models. No new prospectively frozen room-level gate test is inferred from the paired candidate diagnosis.

M3 uses SASRec and GRU4Rec author-level Base families with three fitted initialization seeds each (20261012, 20261013 and 20261014). For each Base, five policy objectives are compared: \emph{Utility} predicts $u_{10}(M,x)-u_{10}(B,x)$, \emph{Difficulty} predicts $-u_{10}(B,x)$, and \emph{Specialist Quality} predicts $u_{10}(M,x)$; all three employ the same regression class and available pre-outcome descriptors. \emph{Uncertainty} uses the negative Base top-two score margin and \emph{History} uses prior interaction count. Uncertainty is a score-margin heuristic, not a calibrated predictive-uncertainty estimator or a reimplementation of an external uncertainty-guided method.

For M3, 12 available descriptors comprise five Base-score summaries (top-two margin, score standard deviation, standardized top score, top score and mean score) and seven history/candidate summaries (prior interaction count, prior TRAIN-known interaction count, number of distinct prior warm authors, maximum warm-author repetition, distinct recent warm authors, candidate-history support share and log candidate count). Twitch uses 14 features: history size, repetition, entropy, historical drift, temporal regularity and a history-complexity index, together with eight Base score-spread, margin and concentration summaries. Feature construction, model training and prior-work alignment are specified in Supplementary S2 and S7. The corresponding selector estimators are trained separately within each task; none is transferred between Twitch and M3.

\subsection{Experimental Protocols}
\label{sec:exp-freeze}
\label{sec:exp-regimes}

\paragraph{Training, out-of-fold estimation and policy freeze.}
All incumbent checkpoints and specialist score definitions are fixed before their corresponding TEST comparisons. Twitch uses five-fold DEV out-of-fold (OOF) utility estimation on its filtered DEV cohort, followed by DEV-frozen Utility threshold selection and a single held-out TEST evaluation. Original KuaiLive pairs the same trained checkpoint and raw score functions over sampled/full candidate protocols; its subsequent candidate analysis reuses already examined TEST interactions.

Each of the six M3 Base checkpoints is selected on the common sampled-candidate DEV screening panel specified above, while gate development and TEST scoring use full-active candidates. Separately for each checkpoint, five user-group-disjoint DEV OOF folds estimate the three supervised gate objectives. Each of the five policy scores—including the two deterministic heuristics—is threshold-calibrated to maximize DEV OOF incremental ranking value subject to at most $30\%$ DEV Memory invocations. The score comparison is inclusive, with all events tied at the selected threshold counted together. Final selectors and thresholds are then fixed before TEST.

The five M3 selection objectives consequently share a \emph{development calibration constraint}, but their realized TEST invocation fractions may differ and can exceed $30\%$. The frozen policies are applied pointwise, without recalibration or batch-level TEST top-count matching. The original Twitch retrospective equal-count Difficulty/Oracle comparisons and the original KuaiLive retrospective candidate diagnostics have distinct inferential status (Table~\ref{tab:exp-methods}).

\paragraph{Forward-time information boundary.}
M3 evaluation proceeds chronologically. Earlier observed interactions—including preceding TEST-period events, whether or not those events belong to the sampled TEST scoring cohort—can enter later users' histories once observed. No current or future target is used for Base/Memory scores, the observable gate features, candidate membership or threshold selection. Base predictions, Memory rankings and policy decisions are recorded before the corresponding TEST target labels are revealed for metric evaluation. Candidate rank ties use a deterministic author-identity convention. Exact source timestamp, event-identity and tie-handling specifications are provided in Supplementary S1--S2.

\paragraph{Paired candidate-regime evaluation.}
For the original KuaiLive room task, the same user history, target and fixed raw scorers are evaluated on the sampled-active subset $S$ and full-active set $F$. The room/streamer Base standardizes branch scores using a candidate-set reference that may change with the regime. Thus, for event $i$ we define the paired change
\begin{equation}
D_i =
\big[u_{10}(M,x_i^{\mathrm{full}})-u_{10}(B,x_i^{\mathrm{full}})\big]
-
\big[u_{10}(M,x_i^{\mathrm{sampled}})-u_{10}(B,x_i^{\mathrm{sampled}})\big].
\label{eq:paired-regime-shift}
\end{equation}
To describe the contribution of candidate membership versus the reference used for branch-score standardization, let $g_i(C,R)$ denote the specialist-minus-Base utility with ranked candidates $C\in\{S,F\}$ and standardization reference $R\in\{S,F\}$. Their order-averaged allocations are
\begin{align}
A_{C,i}&=\tfrac12\big[g_i(F,S)-g_i(S,S)+g_i(F,F)-g_i(S,F)\big],\nonumber\\
A_{R,i}&=\tfrac12\big[g_i(S,F)-g_i(S,S)+g_i(F,F)-g_i(F,S)\big].
\label{eq:factorial-allocations}
\end{align}
The identity $A_{C,i}+A_{R,i}=g_i(F,F)-g_i(S,S)=D_i$ provides a within-protocol descriptive accounting. The membership term combines candidate number, composition and competition rather than identifying a candidate-count-only causal effect. Three checkpoint pairs and nine sampled draws on the same examined cohort support a sensitivity analysis in Supplementary S5 and S8.

\paragraph{Exploratory candidate-supervision and relationship-source comparisons.}
A subsequent M3 development analysis uses the same 50,049 full-active author-ranking DEV events from 12,628 users for the primary frozen SASRec and GRU4Rec seeds. Its sampled evaluation retains the observed author among 575 sampled eligible authors solely to construct retrospective NDCG supervision; that outcome-dependent construction is neither a deployed online candidate generator nor a decision-time selector feature. Holding the Base, fixed Memory and twelve full-active pre-outcome descriptors constant, a five-outer by four-inner user-disjoint procedure separately fits and calibrates policies on sampled labels ($S\!\rightarrow\!F$) or full-active labels ($F\!\rightarrow\!F$). A regime-adapted condition also uses full-active labels from outer-training users and is therefore not label-free adaptation. All outer evaluations use identical full-active events, with an inner-DEV maximum of 30\% Memory calls and naturally realized outer invocation rates. These exploratory fits use 120 boosting iterations, distinct from the 200-iteration predictors of the originally frozen M3 TEST; the other stated capacity and calibration settings are documented in Supplementary S10. Further source comparisons replace Full Memory with its Long-only, Short-only, training-popularity or history-score-placement components; a six-feature candidate-plus-Base panel examines dependence on direct relationship descriptors. Two thousand paired user-cluster bootstrap draws provide descriptive intervals. The user-group-disjoint development folds evaluate transfer to held-out users within the development period, rather than a newly prospective time-forward deployment cohort. These post-primary exploratory analyses do not form an additional independent TEST or extend the original preregistered testing family; details appear in Supplementary S10.

\paragraph{Time-preserving relationship-identity control.}
To distinguish historical-source composition from correct viewer--creator associations, a further exploratory study freezes 1,024 author-ranking requests from 992 users within the same M3 DEV cohort, stratified into 16 cells by pretarget time and history activity and excluding the earlier 128-request feasibility pilot. The original Full and Long-only scores are reproduced event by event under both candidate protocols. A co-active historical null exchanges author labels between different viewers within one-hour source intervals, preserving each viewer's interaction times and counts, each author's hourly activity and source-time eligibility. An exchange is applied to a request only after its source hour has fully closed; the current hour remains unmodified. Three independently seeded null assignments (20261014--20261016) preserve identical full-active and retrospective sampled candidate rosters. The primary comparison is Full Memory NDCG@10 on identical full-active candidates, with 2,000 user-cluster bootstrap resamples per null seed. These null assignments are alternative perturbations on the same development requests, not independent cohorts or a prospective causal experiment (Supplementary S10).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:exp-statistics}

The primary endpoint is event-level NDCG@10 (Eq.~\eqref{eq:event-ndcg}); secondary endpoints are HR@10 and the realized specialist invocation fraction. Comparisons are paired by event, with user-level dependence retained in resampling. Target-specific visibility states (Eq.~\eqref{eq:diagnostic-states}) and recurrence diagnostics are assigned retrospectively and never used as selector inputs. Random@budget expectations and outcome-using Oracle references are descriptive, not prospective learned policies.

Twitch TEST confidence intervals are based on 5,000 user-paired bootstrap replicates; original KuaiLive candidate-regime intervals use 3,000 paired user resamples. For the M3 cohort of 47,575 author requests from 12,533 users, 5,000 user-cluster paired bootstrap samples yield 95\% percentile intervals, and 5,000 user-cluster sign-randomization replicates yield one-sided $p$-values under the stated approximate sign-symmetry null. The predeclared M3 primary family contains Utility versus (i) Always Base, (ii) Difficulty and (iii) Specialist Quality; Holm's procedure \cite{holm1979} adjusts these three contrasts \emph{within each} Base--seed fit. The six fits share the same TEST users and do not imply six independent population tests or familywise control across all 18 contrasts. Utility versus Uncertainty, History and unconditional Memory are additional comparisons, not members of that preregistered three-contrast family. Inference is conditional on the fixed models, candidate rules, cohort and selection policies. The subsequent M3 development comparisons use 2,000 paired user-cluster bootstrap replicates and are exploratory; their intervals do not add to the original M3 TEST Holm family.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp-implementation}

The original KuaiLive room/creator Base uses 64-dimensional ReChorus SASRec branches with one attention layer, four heads and a maximum input length of 50. LiveRec uses a context length of 16 with context and repeat modules. The M3 Base models use 64-dimensional author embeddings and length-16 histories, with one self-attention block for SASRec and one recurrent layer for GRU4Rec. Twitch Utility and the three M3 supervised selector objectives use histogram gradient-boosting regression with 200 iterations, learning rate $0.05$, depth $3$, minimum leaf size $50$ and an $\ell_2$ penalty of $1$. Training and deterministic preprocessing details, full component specifications, six-fit registry and event/metric tie rules are provided in Supplementary S1--S3 and S7--S8. Observed Memory-call rates and warmed scoring time are reported separately because one is not a substitute for the other.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results connect three aspects of relationship knowledge: its conditional ranking contribution, the decision value of predicting that contribution and its dependence on the candidate regime. Developmental diagnostics and execution-path measurements clarify their interpretation.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Utility of Relationship Memory}
\label{sec:results-heterogeneity}

The ranking contribution of relationship memory is heterogeneous rather than uniformly positive. On 44,221 Twitch held-out streamer-ranking requests, unconditional Memory obtains NDCG@10 $0.53980$, below LiveRec Base at $0.58211$ (paired difference $-0.04232$, 95\% CI $[-0.04521,-0.03950]$). Yet the negative aggregate masks a substantial positive contribution for previously encountered creators outside the incumbent's visible input window.

Figure~\ref{fig:results-evidence-states} and Table~\ref{tab:results-evidence-states} show that Memory improves NDCG@10 by $+0.22045$ on the 5,539 \emph{recoverable-but-unrepresented} TEST events. Its contribution is $-0.01935$ on 24,285 represented events and $-0.18215$ on 14,397 unavailable events. These target-defined groups identify the conditional pattern after the outcome is observed; the online selector does not receive their labels. The recoverable-group gain demonstrates that older observed relationships can add ranking information even when the same fixed specialist underperforms on average.

In the distinct KuaiLive-M3 author-ranking TEST, Memory reaches NDCG@10 $0.34679$ on 47,575 events, exceeding each of six fitted SASRec and GRU4Rec incumbents ($0.25800$--$0.26065$). For the primary SASRec fit its advantage is $+0.08879$. Thus, the sign of aggregate Memory--Base value changes across the two held-out tasks. Because Base design, ranking unit, candidates and time protocol differ together, this contrast characterizes incumbent- and setting-relative knowledge value without assigning the difference to one isolated cause.

\subsection{Decision Value of Predicting Relative Utility}
\label{sec:results-decision}

Given heterogeneous specialist value, the relevant decision signal is the predicted improvement of \emph{this} Memory over \emph{this} Base, rather than predicted Base difficulty alone. On Twitch, a DEV-frozen Utility rule selects Memory on 6,650 of 44,221 TEST requests ($15.04\%$) and improves NDCG@10 from Base $0.58211$ to $0.58983$ (paired gain $+0.00772$, 95\% CI $[+0.00641,+0.00903]$). The nondeployable outcome-informed Oracle yields $0.64806$ NDCG@10 at the same call count. These observations establish decision relevance while leaving considerable predictive headroom.

\subsection{Candidate-Regime Dependence of Relative Utility}
\label{sec:results-regimes}

The candidate environment is part of the value being estimated. On the original KuaiLive \emph{room}-ranking task, 10,222 identical user--time--target events yield opposite Memory--Base results when the same fixed raw ranker scores are evaluated under sampled-active and full-active candidates. Membership combines candidate count, identity and rank competition; the contrast does not isolate a causal effect of list length.

\subsection{Relationship Sources and Observable Signals}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The source of historical knowledge is also relevant to its measured ranking value. For matched M3 development events, Full Memory is better than Long-only under full-active candidates ($0.343509$ versus $0.333231$ NDCG@10), whereas Long-only ranks ahead under retrospective sampled candidates ($0.773602$ versus $0.668498$; Table~\ref{tab:results-m3-dev-source}). Under original twelve-feature policies, Long-only incurs a larger candidate-supervision transfer loss than Full Memory ($0.010605$ versus $0.006159$ for SASRec and $0.010262$ versus $0.004970$ for GRU4Rec). In six-feature candidate-plus-Base controls, the Full-Memory transfer losses decrease to $0.001814$ and $0.001607$; these feature panels differ in information content, so the contrast does not identify an isolated causal mechanism. Short-only transfer also produces a large change in actual Memory calls; popularity and history-placement controls have near-zero invocation floors. A matched user-cluster analysis of fixed source utility clarifies the candidate-regime dependence: Full minus Long-only averages $+0.010278$ under $F$ (95\% CI $[+0.007906,+0.012668]$) and $-0.105104$ under retrospective $S$ (CI $[-0.108326,-0.101683]$), giving a paired source--regime interaction of $+0.115382$ (CI $[+0.111724,+0.119065]$). This is an aggregate ranking-value interaction rather than pervasive event-level sign reversal: 46,462 requests have zero Full--Long difference under at least one regime, and only 66 exhibit strictly opposite nonzero source-gap signs. A nine-cell decomposition attributes $+0.100885$ of the $+0.115382$ net interaction (87.4\%) to events with a zero source gap in at least one regime, consistent with a substantial sampled top-ten support effect. This motivates examining relation identity under a \emph{fixed} candidate environment rather than interpreting the source interaction as independent causal evidence.

On 1,024 stratified M3 DEV requests, valid historical-author nulls retaining source-hour author activity and user interaction times remain useful rankers but score below the original Full Memory under the identical full-active author universe (Table~\ref{tab:results-m3-dev-relation-identity}). Across three prespecified null seeds, original-minus-null NDCG@10 lies between $+0.032858$ and $+0.035544$, with positive paired user-cluster intervals. The source swaps modify approximately 78.6\% of eligible history edges and also alter repeated-pair and higher-order relational structure; the contrast measures sensitivity to a strong time- and degree-preserving identity perturbation, not a uniquely identified causal effect of one relational feature. None of these development results is an additional independent TEST (Supplementary S10).

The M3 Utility rules realize $27.72$--$29.21\%$ Memory calls on TEST after the DEV call-limit calibration. History reaches $35.47\%$ on TEST, illustrating that a frozen pointwise rule need not preserve its DEV call fraction. Invocation rates alone do not measure full-path computation.

In a matched, exploratory nested M3 DEV sensitivity analysis, raising the inner specialist-call limit from $30\%$ to $50\%$ increases SASRec policy NDCG@10 from $0.302658$ to $0.319654$ and GRU4Rec from $0.303093$ to $0.320478$; at a $90\%$ limit both approach $0.34105$. An accompanying matched DEV comparison finds Direct relative-utility and separate two-stage utility prediction close, whereas predicting Base difficulty or Memory quality alone gives lower NDCG over $10\%$, $30\%$ and $50\%$ inner limits. An additional decision-value analysis evaluates $J_{\lambda}(\pi)=V_F(\pi)-\lambda q(\pi)$, where $q$ is the \emph{actual outer} Memory-call fraction and $\lambda$ is a hypothetical disutility measured in expected event NDCG per specialist invocation. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Relative Value of Relationship Knowledge}
\label{sec:discussion-operational-value}

Explicit relationship history is a potential source of knowledge, but its value for recommendation emerges through comparison with what an incumbent ranker can already achieve. The Twitch results illustrate this distinction: although Memory is weaker than Base in aggregate, it contributes substantial ranking value when the observed target belongs to earlier relationship history outside the incumbent's input window. Such recoverable history is neither uniformly useful nor wholly redundant. The relevant phenomenon is the conditional complementarity of a particular historical signal to a particular trained model, rather than an unconditional benefit of longer histories.

The M3 author-ranking results add an important counterpart: Memory is stronger than the six evaluated Base fits in aggregate, despite weaker aggregate Memory performance on Twitch. These task-specific comparisons show why the standalone quality of a knowledge source and its marginal value are separate quantities. The definition $\Delta_M=u_{10}(M)-u_{10}(B)$ makes the incumbent part of the measurement, allowing positive and negative contributions to be analyzed with the same criterion. For knowledge-based recommendation, this provides an interpretable evaluation of an auxiliary evidence source even when its representation is not jointly learned with the incumbent. It complements representation- and fusion-oriented research \cite{xie2026mhgfcl,chen2026memrec} by connecting knowledge use to a concrete change in ranking decisions.

\subsection{Candidate Context Defines the Value Being Predicted}
\label{sec:discussion-candidate-regimes}

Ranking value is also defined by the alternatives against which a target competes. The paired original KuaiLive room analysis holds the observed user--time--target event and fixed raw Base and Memory scores constant while changing the eligible candidate regime. Its reversal of mean Memory--Base utility demonstrates that a label representing the specialist's contribution under one candidate universe need not retain its sign under another. Consequently, training and evaluating a knowledge selector requires a clearly specified ranking unit and candidate-generation process. The value target is aligned with the decision only when the comparison is made over the alternatives actually relevant to that decision.

Prior work establishes that sampled recommendation metrics can distort full-ranking model comparisons \cite{krichene2020kdd}. The present analysis identifies a related consequence for specialist valuation: candidate-dependent evaluation affects the supervision used to estimate \emph{whether a particular knowledge source should replace an incumbent}. In the subsequent matched M3 development analysis, candidate-aligned labels also lead to higher downstream full-ranking selection quality than sampled-label supervision, directly connecting the label definition to a choice consequence under specified invocation constraints. The source ablations show a positive paired Full-versus-Long candidate-regime interaction, with most of the signed total attributable to events whose top-ten source gap is zero in at least one regime. The time-preserving identity-null experiment adds a complementary contrast under \emph{unchanged full-active candidates}: the correctly arranged historical relationships outperform three useful, degree- and activity-preserving randomized arrangements on a stratified M3 development sample. This strengthens the knowledge-specific interpretation beyond sampled-evaluation distortion, while the strong intervention, disrupted higher-order patterns and absence of a new independent TEST prevent attributing the entire difference to a single causal relation-identity mechanism. Further request-level accounting shows that the downstream selection effect occurs because sampled- and full-supervised policies select different requests, even with almost unchanged total Memory calls. The crossed room-ranking analysis distinguishes candidate membership from the score-standardization reference. Most of the measured difference is associated with membership, which jointly changes candidate number, identity and competition. Together with the matched M3 supervision-transfer evidence, this shows why candidate definition must be part of the specification of relationship-knowledge value and its downstream decision target.

\subsection{Predicted Marginal Value as a Selection Signal}
\label{sec:discussion-selective-decisions}

Estimating the expected incremental utility of a fixed specialist connects the valuation problem to a decision: use Memory where the available pre-outcome information indicates that its ranking is preferable to Base. This is consistent with established post-hoc expert allocation and regression-based learning-to-defer principles \cite{narasimhan2022posthoc,mao2024regression}. The distinctive question in our setting is what should be predicted for an explicit relationship-memory ranker under a fixed candidate protocol. Base difficulty, Memory's standalone quality and a score-margin uncertainty proxy each summarize a different property of the request; none alone directly represents the utility difference between the two complete rankings.

The held-out Twitch and independently frozen M3 selection studies show that this difference is a useful decision target for the tested invocation-constrained policies. The M3 finding is particularly informative because Utility improves both ranking quality and observed Memory-call frequency relative to each of four specified alternative objectives across two incumbent families and three seeds. The common TEST user cohort and different realized call rates define the statistical and resource scope of that evidence. A predicted relative benefit can thus be informative without perfectly recovering the outcome-dependent benefit of each individual event, as the remaining gap to hindsight Oracle selection illustrates.

The choice of a direct utility regressor should not be confused with a distinct population decision principle. By linearity of conditional expectation, estimating $\mathbb{E}[u_{10}(M)-u_{10}(B)\mid Z]$ directly and subtracting separate conditional estimates of the two rankers' utilities target the same quantity. Their practical behavior can differ through finite-sample estimation, calibration and computation. The exploratory nested-DEV comparison (Supplementary S7.7) finds similar constrained selection quality from both estimators, with small incumbent-dependent differences. An additional matched development analysis over 10\%, 30\% and 50\% inner invocation limits likewise finds close Direct and two-stage decisions, while predictors of Base difficulty and Memory standalone quality produce lower selection value (Supplementary S10). This implementation sensitivity does not establish superiority over existing general learning-to-defer algorithms or alter the frozen TEST comparisons.

\subsection{Invocation Allocation and System Cost Are Distinct}
\label{sec:discussion-validity}

The M3 decision evidence reveals a distinction between selecting Memory calls and establishing that selection is operationally preferable. Within the development-calibrated invocation regime, Utility allocates fewer observed Memory calls and obtains better ranking quality than the tested alternative selectors. Yet unconditional Memory has higher absolute TEST NDCG@10 than every selective M3 policy. In the separate warmed DEV scoring replay, Always Memory also takes less time per event than the complete Utility path, because constructing decision features and evaluating the gate adds work even when Memory is skipped. Neither a lower call fraction nor improved comparison against other constrained selectors is therefore sufficient evidence of lower end-to-end system cost.

This observation helps specify when conditional knowledge use would have a practical role. External memory access might be subject to independently measured price, permissions, availability or scarce computational capacity. Under such conditions, the relevant decision criterion combines the predicted ranking gain with the cost of obtaining the specialist's output. In the absence of a measured scarcity of that kind, unconditional Memory is a strong alternative on the present M3 task. The exploratory M3 call-quality frontier further shows that removing the invocation restriction produces near-universal Memory selection, a useful empirical boundary on when a learned selective choice is warranted. The idealized development-set shadow-price analysis makes the conditional choice criterion explicit: different permitted invocation rates maximize expected ranking utility at different hypothetical per-call costs, while Always Memory remains preferred at zero cost. The corresponding $\lambda$ is not a measured monetary or latency price, and the available point estimates do not justify inference about net production benefit. The study consequently establishes relative-utility-guided allocation under a \emph{stipulated} knowledge-use rule; a production deployment claim requires a workload-matched comparison of complete scoring paths and actual resource constraints.

\subsection{Transferability and Implications for Knowledge-Based Recommendation}
\label{sec:discussion-implications}

Three aspects of the evaluation delimit transferability. First, Twitch ranks streamers, the original KuaiLive protocol ranks rooms, and M3 ranks persistent authors; differences in aggregate relative utility cannot be assigned to one changed factor across these nonexchangeable tasks. Second, historical visibility states and the room candidate-regime decomposition are retrospective explanations, whereas the Twitch and M3 policies use decision-time observable descriptors and frozen selection rules. Third, the six M3 fitted incumbents share the same chronologically evaluated TEST users; their agreement supports robustness across fitted Bases rather than independent population replication. The original within-fit Holm inference and the distinction between development call calibration and realized TEST calls remain the appropriate basis for interpreting selection gains.

These findings suggest a general evaluation discipline for auxiliary knowledge in recommendation. An additional evidence source can be assessed through its value \emph{relative to the intended incumbent and candidate environment}, examined for conditional complementarities, and then evaluated as a pre-outcome decision signal under an explicitly justified invocation regime. Such a discipline makes knowledge selection more interpretable: it asks not only what information an auxiliary model contains, but which ranking decisions that information actually improves. Determining whether selective use is economically warranted is a further empirical question about the full system, rather than an automatic consequence of fewer specialist invocations.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study examines relationship memory as a conditional ranking resource whose incremental value depends on the trained incumbent and eligible candidates. On Twitch, older recoverable viewer--creator interactions explain a substantial positive specialist contribution within an otherwise negative aggregate Memory--Base comparison. The distinct KuaiLive-M3 author-ranking task instead exhibits a positive aggregate Memory advantage across six fitted incumbents. In paired original KuaiLive room-ranking events, changing from sampled-active to full-active candidates reverses the measured Memory--Base advantage without changing the evaluated targets. A separate matched exploratory author-ranking development analysis shows that candidate-aligned supervision changes which requests receive the fixed specialist under nearly equal invocation fractions and improves their subsequent full-active choice quality. Paired historical-source comparisons reveal a candidate-regime interaction concentrated in changes of top-ten support; an additional time- and activity-preserving source-randomization study on stratified M3 development requests shows that the authentic viewer--creator relation arrangement retains higher full-candidate ranking value than useful randomized controls. This intervention-sensitive evidence does not constitute independent confirmation or identify one isolated relational cause. Together, these observations establish the importance of specifying the incumbent, available knowledge source and candidate regime when evaluating explicit relationship evidence.

The corresponding decision question is whether that value can be estimated before the outcome. Frozen Twitch and M3 evaluations show that predictions of marginal ranking utility improve specialist allocation relative to the tested alternative objectives under stated invocation-count rules. The result concerns the quality of deciding \emph{which} requests benefit from a fixed knowledge ranker: in M3, Always Memory still provides greater unconstrained ranking accuracy and faster warmed scoring than the selective path. Thus the use of a learned gate requires a separately justified operational constraint. More broadly, incumbent-relative valuation and candidate-aligned evaluation provide an empirically grounded approach to understanding when external relationship knowledge contributes to sequential recommendation.
