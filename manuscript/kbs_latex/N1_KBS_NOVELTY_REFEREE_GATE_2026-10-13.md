## B. Four simulated major reviewer objections and rebuttal limits

### R1 (major): "Is this simply post-hoc L2D with event-NDCG labels?"
**Substantive issue:** Yes, the mathematics follows established two-stage deferral. Manuscript contribution must not be sold as a general new decision rule. **Defensible empirical distinction:** an auxiliary transparent relationship ranker is kept separate from Base; its observed fixed-candidate improvement is measured under heterogeneity and an explicit candidate-regime diagnostic; a pre-outcome estimator is tested in two nonidentical frozen settings. **Residual risk:** empirical packaging may still be judged incremental. A fair modern selector comparison could add needed distinctiveness; N2 must decide.

### R2 (major): "Do budgeted fast–slow recommenders already cover this?"
**Substantive issue:** DS-Frame (2026) is an especially close abstract-level collision: fast base, slower expert, learned selector and controlled budget. **Defensible distinction:** here expert is a fixed transparent relationship memory with task-specific incremental event-NDCG; central study includes target-history complementarity and candidate-indexed supervision. **Residual risk:** these are scientifically coherent but not necessarily sufficient KBS novelty without a head-to-head gate baseline or broader independently verified discovery. Include DS-Frame accurately in N5; its forthcoming CIKM publication status must be reflected correctly.

### R3 (major): "Why defer at all when Memory is better and faster?"
**Substantive issue:** Under measured M3 mean NDCG and warmed scoring time, a chooser is not superior to always using Memory. **Correct scope:** conditional quality under a *stipulated* invocation-count constraint. If external permissions/prices or resource scarcity are not demonstrated, utility-gated selection remains a decision experiment, not an efficiency improvement. **Residual risk:** this may be a material application-motivation defect, requiring new evidence or narrower scientific claim. N2 is a go/no-go experimental decision point.

### R4 (major): "Aren't some of your comparisons unfair or post-hoc?"
**Substantive issue:** Twitch Difficulty is retrospectively count matched; M3 all five policies use DEV max-30% constraints but distinct TEST rates; original KuaiLive room candidate diagnostic reuses examined TEST; all six M3 fits share one TEST cohort. **Correct scope:** faithful statuses and users must be disclosed, and the chosen fixed rules must never be retuned after G5. RQ2 doesn't establish independent prospective generalization. **Residual risk:** no exact resource-equal baseline across all algorithms, external original KuaiLive room validation remains restricted; N2 should prioritize if stronger acceptance probability is intended.
