## 1. Single research question and falsifiable thesis

**Research question:** Does a mismatch between the candidate regime used to label the incumbent-relative value of fixed historical relationship Memory and the full-active candidate regime used for actual ranking change the resulting frozen knowledge-use policies' allocation and weighted full-candidate NDCG@10?

**Core object:** With fixed Base `B`, fixed historical relationship Memory `M`, the observed target-based offline ranking functional `u10`, and sampled `S` or full `F` candidate sets, `Delta_r=u10(M;C_r)-u10(B;C_r)`. Separate `S`- and `F`-supervised pretarget `Z` policies are DEV calibrated and evaluated on the same full-active candidates. Do not write them as identical fitting/calibration interventions or a strictly matched-call causal contrast.

**Falsifiers / negative outcomes that would change the inference:** (i) F and S policy full-F quality are indistinguishable on separately frozen additional events; (ii) policy choices rarely diverge on common DEV requests; (iii) any observed policy difference is wholly attributable to unmeasured cost/calibration freedom rather than a useful candidate-alignment design lesson; (iv) legitimate corrected sampled-only supervision matches F; (v) the effect is merely the direct, obvious sampled-metric bias + standard defer application without additional useful knowledge-system implications. Current evidence resolves (i) only for the tested complete policies, describes (ii) on exploratory DEV, and leaves (iii)–(v) materially open.

## 2. Narrow contribution statement (provisional, evidence bounded)

1. **Decision-target diagnosis.** A paired same-checkpoint room-ranking comparison demonstrates that the observed incremental ranking value of a fixed relationship source is indexed by the evaluated candidate roster; because candidate identity and competition shift together, this diagnostic is not a candidate-count causal theorem.
2. **Frozen-policy consequence.** Two separately frozen candidate-supervision policies on 30,000 additional same-period M3 author targets show better full-active population-weighted NDCG for F relative to original uncorrected sampled-label S, with *lower naturally realized population-weighted specialist-call fractions*. The shared-DEV switch accounting explains the changed allocation descriptively; the result neither identifies a pure label-only effect at exactly matched calls nor validates production cost savings.

No third independent contribution is named. H2 is not an explanation of H1's causal mechanism and is excluded from headline contributions. Memory architecture is deliberately not proposed as new.
