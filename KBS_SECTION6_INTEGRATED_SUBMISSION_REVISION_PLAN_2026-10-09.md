**Mandatory measurement semantics:** Twitch recoverable states use *observed target* (not gate input). Same-event candidate switches adjust ranking difficulty/composition and reference statistics jointly; even 2×2 attribution isolates a scoring-protocol factor, **not** physical intervention on catalog size. User-paired confidence intervals condition on frozen predictions, masks and selected thresholds; same users across checkpoint seeds are not independent data samples. Oracle is only the hindsight best exactly \(m\) switches between these two fixed rankings in the evaluated batch; Difficulty exact-\(m\) is batch-topK, unlike the pointwise frozen Utility selector.

## 8. Final editorial conclusions and decisions

1. **The established novelty is the scientific starting point, not an unresolved question.** The paper studies operational marginal ranking value of fixed relationship evidence relative to a designated Base and candidate universe; conditional structure and limited selective decision benefits are supported under bounded protocols. Gate architectures and sampling-metric theory are related foundations, not new claims to outperform wholesale.
2. **The real nonoptional work is manuscript integrity:** experiment provenance, cross-chapter roles, DEV/TEST information boundaries, confidence interval semantics, and real S1–S9 supplements.
3. **The best return on existing data** is S4 Memory components and S7 OOF common-budget profiles, after source integrity checks; no fresh model training required.
4. **P2 KuaiLive repeats and more Gate targets** remain rational but conditional extensions. Existing same-checkpoint KuaiLive P0/P1 and Twitch frozen TEST must not be discounted solely because those optional experiments were not performed.
5. **Keep Section 6's narrative intact:** heterogeneous value → predicted decision benefit → operating-context dependence → explanatory sensitivity → bounded computational cost. Begin final Discussion only after that evidence structure and supplement are independently source-checked.
