## 1. Recommended submission order

### Target A — Expert Systems with Applications (ESWA)

**Recommendation:** first choice if the manuscript is written as an applied-AI paper with a clear real-world live-streaming problem, strong mechanism validation, and practical inference analysis.

Why it fits:

- applied AI / intelligent systems scope is highly compatible with the paper;
- live-stream recommendation work with repeated behaviors has already appeared in ESWA, showing direct topical acceptance;
- large publication volume;
- publisher-level handling statistics are fast relative to many AI journals;
- current paper has both a method contribution and a concrete application setting, which is important for ESWA desk screening.

Current public metrics / signals:

- annual article volume reported by LetPub/Peeref: **~4059**;
- publisher-reported median: **5 days to first decision**;
- **62 days to decision after review**;
- **147 days submission to acceptance**;
- **7 days acceptance to online publication**;
- 2025 upgraded CAS table commonly reports **Computer Science 1st tier**;
- SCIE / JCR Q1.

Main risk:

- very fast desk screening; a manuscript framed too abstractly as “generic gating” may be rejected before review;
- the introduction must make the live-streaming application problem, dynamic availability, repeat relationships, temporal shift and real deployment cost immediately visible.

Recommended framing for ESWA:

> Conditional specialist utility for explicit memory in dynamic live-stream recommendation, with strong identity controls, temporal regime analysis and inference-cost decomposition.

Relevant sources:

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/expert-systems-with-applications
- https://www.letpub.com.cn/index.php?journalid=2799&page=journalapp&view=detail
- https://www.peeref.com/journals/2799/expert-systems-with-applications
- live-stream repeated-behavior article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.128217

### Target B — Knowledge-Based Systems (KBS)

**Recommendation:** second choice; arguably the cleanest conceptual fit if the manuscript emphasizes explicit memory as a knowledge specialist and the learned relative-utility selector.

Why it fits:

- journal scope explicitly includes **recommender systems and e-service personalization**;
- strong fit for knowledge-based / intelligent decision support framing;
- high publication volume;
- method-oriented framing is more natural here than in ESWA.

Current public metrics / signals:

- annual article volume: **~1952**;
- Elsevier-reported / mirrored metrics: **~90 days first decision**, **157 days submission to acceptance**, **8 days acceptance to publication**;
- 2025 upgraded CAS table commonly reports **Computer Science 2nd tier**;
- SCIE / JCR Q1.

Main risk:

- reviewers may expect the “memory specialist” to be more clearly formalized as knowledge representation / knowledge use rather than a purely heuristic feature cache;
- theoretical positioning of conditional specialist utility must be clear.

Recommended framing for KBS:

> A hybrid knowledge/sequential recommendation framework in which explicit relationship memory is a specialist whose conditional relative utility is learned and selectively exploited.

Relevant sources:

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/knowledge-based-systems
- https://www.letpub.com.cn/index.php?journalid=5495&page=journalapp&view=detail
- https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/knowledge-based-systems/0950-7051

### Target C — Information Processing & Management (IPM)

**Recommendation:** strong alternative if the paper is written around information processing, uncertainty/confidence, user-state heterogeneity, and adaptive use of historical information.

Why it fits:

- publishes work at the intersection of computing, information science, social computing and recommendation;
- the paper’s question “when is long-term information useful beyond sequential state?” can be positioned naturally;
- fast publisher-level handling statistics.

Current public metrics / signals:

- annual article volume: **~443**;
- **5 days time to first decision**;
- **96 days review time**;
- **147 days submission to acceptance**;
- 2025 upgraded CAS table commonly reports **Computer Science 1st tier**;
- SCIE / SSCI, JCR Q1.

Main risk:

- publication volume is materially lower than ESWA/KBS;
- pure recommender-engineering framing is less ideal than an information-behavior / information-processing narrative.

Recommended framing for IPM:

> Heterogeneous value of long-term user information under dynamic recommendation regimes, and confidence-guided selective use of explicit relationship memory.

Relevant sources:

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/information-processing-and-management
- https://www.letpub.com.cn/index.php?journalid=3564&page=journalapp&view=detail

### Target D — Information Sciences

**Recommendation:** backup target when emphasizing general conditional-utility learning and intelligent information systems rather than live-streaming application alone.

Current public signals:

- annual article volume reported by LetPub: **~894**;
- 2025 upgraded CAS table commonly reports **Computer Science 2nd tier**;
- broad AI / information-systems scope.

Strength:

- sufficiently broad to accept the conditional-utility formulation;
- substantial annual volume.

Risk:

- novelty expectations can be high and the paper must be generalized beyond one application;
- the current manuscript would benefit from an external dataset before targeting this venue.

Source:

- https://www.letpub.com.cn/index.php?journalid=3567&page=journalapp&view=detail

### Target E — Neurocomputing

**Recommendation:** high-volume backup if speed / throughput matter and the manuscript is made more model-centric.

Current public signals:

- current LetPub listing: CAS **2nd tier**;
- SCIE;
- reported review cycle roughly several months;
- very large historical publication volume.

Risk:

- less direct topical fit than ESWA/KBS for the current mechanism/application story;
- would require more emphasis on architecture / learning mechanism and less on live-stream domain interpretation.

Source:

- https://www.letpub.com.cn/index.php?page=journalapp&searchname=Neurocomputing&view=search

## 2. Recommended ranking under the user’s constraints

The user’s constraints are: CAS 1st/2nd tier, fast review, high publication volume, and strong fit.

### Overall ranking

1. **Expert Systems with Applications** — best combination of fit + volume + official speed signal.
2. **Knowledge-Based Systems** — strongest conceptual/method fit; slightly slower than ESWA but still high volume.
3. **Information Processing & Management** — strong scientific fit and fast handling, but lower article volume.
4. **Information Sciences** — broad and credible, but less directly aligned and likely needs stronger external generalization.
5. **Neurocomputing** — high-volume fallback, less clean narrative fit.

### If only one journal is selected now

> **Submit first to Expert Systems with Applications.**

Rationale:

- the paper has a strong real-world application;
- ESWA already publishes live-stream recommendation;
- the completed identity, temporal, collision and latency analyses make the paper look more like a mature applied-AI study than a narrow recommender benchmark;
- annual volume is exceptionally large;
- official handling medians are favorable.

### If the manuscript becomes more theoretical / knowledge-centric

> Move **Knowledge-Based Systems** to first choice.

This would be appropriate if the final introduction and method sections foreground “conditional specialist utility” as a general knowledge-integration framework rather than live-stream recommendation as the primary application story.

## 3. Journal-specific manuscript adjustments

### For ESWA

Must emphasize:

- realistic live-streaming application;
- current-room dynamics and legal active-at-time candidates;
- repeated user–streamer relationship memory;
- temporal regime change;
- systems latency and deployability;
- clear practical consequence of selecting when to use memory.

Do not over-emphasize abstract recommender theory at the expense of application substance.

### For KBS

Must emphasize:

- explicit relationship memory as a knowledge source;
- base vs specialist decomposition;
- formal conditional relative utility;
- selector as a learned decision mechanism for when knowledge should override/augment sequential representation;
- generalization beyond live-streaming as a knowledge-integration pattern.

### For IPM

Must emphasize:

- heterogeneous informational value of history;
- user-state and confidence signals;
- dynamic information environments;
- information-processing trade-offs under temporal shift;
- behavioral interpretation of repeated relationships.
